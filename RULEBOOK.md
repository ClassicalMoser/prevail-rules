### How to use this book

**Never played before?** Part I is all you need. It sets up your first battle and walks you through it. Twenty minutes, and it is easier with the pieces in front of you.

**Playing already?** Part II is the full ruleset, numbered so you can find and cite any rule. Part III has a one-page summary, a list of the situations that cause arguments, and the unit roster.

Throughout, a reference like §4.3.2 points to a numbered rule in Part II. You can ignore them on a first read.

This book states the game as designed. A small mark like means the digital edition does not yet play that rule as written, or that the rule itself is still open. The appendices at the back say what differs and what would settle it. At the table, play the rule as printed.

# PART I — LEARN TO PLAY

## 1. The short version

Two armies stand on a grid. You each command some troops and one general.

Every round, you both play a card face down and turn it over together. The card says what orders you are allowed to give. You give them. Units walk forward, bump into each other, and fight.

Fighting costs you cards.

**When a player's hand of cards runs out, that player loses.** Your hand is your army's will to fight; when it is gone, the battle is over.

That is the whole game. The rest is how it plays.

## 2. Set up your first battle

**The board.** Put it between you. It is wider than it is deep; you each sit at a long edge. It needs no markings — nothing in this game is described by naming a square. Leave it bare for your first battle. Terrain, scenarios and the bidding for position (§2.5–2.8, §3.2–3.6) come later.

**Your armies.** For a first game, use these two. Each carries exactly the Morale a Mini army needs (§3.1.3).

| Rome             |     | Carthage               |     |
| ---------------- | --- | ---------------------- | --- |
| Manipular Legion | ×4  | Punic Citizen Spearmen | ×4  |
| Equites          | ×1  | Iberian Cavalry        | ×1  |

Their numbers are in §15. For a mirror match, both of you can take the Roman list.

**Your cards.** Take eight command cards: two showing initiative 1, two showing 2, two showing 3, two showing 4. For a first game, prefer cards whose command says _Move_, and read the small print: a card that names a kind of unit you do not have cannot order anyone. These eight cards are your hand for the whole battle. There is no pile to draw from — what you hold is what you get, and the only way to get spent cards back is to rally, which you will meet in Phase 5.

**Deploy.** White places the whole army first, then Black. Place your units in the three rows nearest your own edge, one to a space. Stay clear of the two columns at each end — those are the open flanks, and nobody starts in them. Point every unit at the enemy. Black deploys seeing where White stands; White moves first in return.<sup>[A5](#a5)</sup>

**Your general.** Put it anywhere in those same three rows. It can share a space with one of your units.

**White has initiative** for the first round, as in chess.<sup>[A5](#a5)</sup>

## 3. Your units

A unit is a whole formation of men — a legion, a squadron of horse, a screen of skirmishers. Not one soldier.

Four numbers tell you what it can do:

|                 |                                         |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Attack**      | how hard it hits                        |
| **Speed**       | how many spaces it can step in one move |
| **Flexibility** | how many times it can turn in one move  |
| **Range**       | how far it can shoot, if at all         |

Three more tell you what it takes to break it. These are **thresholds**: hit a unit hard enough to reach one, and that happens to it. Nothing carries over between blows. There is no damage to track, only where the unit now stands and which way it faces.

|             |                                          |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Retreat** | it gives ground                          |
| **Reverse** | it is spun round to face the wrong way   |
| **Rout**    | it breaks and leaves the battle for good |

They are printed in that order. They usually climb, but not always: a unit's Retreat can be higher than its Reverse, and either can be as high as its Rout.

Finally, **Morale** is what a unit's rout costs you: discard that many cards from your hand. Cheap skirmishers cost nothing. Good troops hurt.

### Facing is the whole game

Every unit points one of eight ways, and you must always be able to tell which. This matters more than any number on the unit.

```
@@FIG facing-p1@@
        ↑  the way it is facing

     ·   ·   ·      ← its three front spaces
     ·  [U]  ·      ← its two flanking spaces
     ·   ·   ·      ← its three back spaces
```

Three things follow from it, and you will use all three in your first game:

- A unit **only ever walks forwards**. To go anywhere else, it turns first.
- A friendly unit helps you fight most when it is **looking at the fight**, and a little when the fight is off to its side.
- Being caught **from behind** does not give the enemy an advantage. It kills you outright.

## 4. Your cards

A card has no rules text. It is a set of printed values, each doing a different job. For your first game you need two of them.

**Initiative, 1 to 4.** Low goes first.

**The command.** What you may order this round — for example _4 units, Move_, or _2 lines, Shoot_. Most commands only reach units standing near your general.

Pick up the other three as you play:

- a **round effect**, a bonus that applies all round to your units near your general;
- **unit support**, which decides how large an army your hand can hold together;
- and a **stat it can be committed for**, letting you throw the card away for +1 at a critical moment.

## 5. The round

Five phases, in this order, every round.

|       | Phase         | Who goes first       |
| ----- | ------------- | -------------------- |
| **1** | Play cards    | both at once         |
| **2** | Move generals | first player         |
| **3** | Give orders   | first player         |
| **4** | Fight         | first player chooses |
| **5** | Clean up      | first player         |

### Phase 1 — Play cards

Both of you pick a card from hand and put it face down. Turn them over together.

**The lower initiative number wins initiative** for this round and goes first in every phase that follows. That makes you the **first player** for the round, and your opponent the **second player**. On a tie, whoever already had it keeps it.

Your card stays face up in front of you all round. You are committed to it.

### Phase 2 — Move generals

The first player goes first, then the second.

Your general moves up to **4 spaces**, any direction, and can walk over and stop on your own units. It cannot enter a space with an enemy unit in it. It can also stay where it is.

Generals never fight. They are there because most cards only reach units standing near them. A general sharing a space with a unit can also move with it when it moves or retreats.

### Phase 3 — Give orders

First player gives **all** their orders and carries them out. Then the second player does the same.

**How many orders you get.** Read your card. _4 units_ means you may order up to four of your units — fewer is fine, and you may give no order at all. _2 lines_ means two orders, each aimed at a whole **line**: your units standing shoulder to shoulder in a row, all facing the same way (or exactly opposite), up to eight of them at once. A line is how you move an army instead of a unit.

A unit cannot be ordered twice in a round, and a unit already locked in a fight cannot move.<sup>[A1](#a1)</sup>

**Moving a unit.** Spend its two allowances in any order you like:

- **Speed** — one point per space stepped. A unit steps only in the direction it faces.
- **Flexibility** — one point per turn. A turn is 45° to either side, or a full about-face.<sup>[A2](#a2)</sup>

You cannot walk through an enemy, and you cannot stop on a friend. You _can_ walk through a friend if your Flexibility and theirs add up to 6 or more.

> _A Manipular Legion has Speed 2, Flexibility 3. It can step two spaces straight ahead. Or turn, step, turn, step. Or step, step, turn, turn, turn. Any order works, so long as it spends no more than two steps and three turns. Two Legions can pass through each other — 3 and 3 make 6 — which is exactly what the manipular lines were drilled to do. Equites (Flexibility 2) cannot pass through a Legion: 2 and 3 make only 5._

**Walking into the enemy.** Step into a space with an enemy in it and you are **engaged**: you both stand in that one space until the fight resolves in Phase 4. What you get depends on which way the two of you end up facing.

| You are facing…               | What happens                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **opposite** the enemy        | Head on. If it is faster than you it may back off; otherwise you fight in Phase 4.             |
| **across** the enemy          | You hit its flank. It is dragged round to face you, cannot back off, and you fight in Phase 4. |
| **the same way** as the enemy | You came from behind. It routs on the spot. No fight at all.                                   |

That last row is why facing is the whole game.

> **Shooting.** If your card's command says _Shoot_ instead of _Move_, your ordered units with Range shoot rather than move. Each picks one enemy in the widening cone of spaces ahead of it. Several units ordered by the same card can gang up on one target: one shoots, the rest add +1 each instead of shooting themselves. Skip this on your first game if your cards let you.

### Phase 4 — Fight

Every engaged space fights. The first player picks the order.

Both units in a fight attack **at the same time**. Both can break. There is no attacker and defender.

For each of them, add up:

|                |                                                 |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| its **Attack** | the number on the unit                          |
| **support**    | see below                                       |
| **+1**         | if that player throws away a card to help it    |
| any bonus      | from the card its player has in play this round |

**Support** often tips a fight. Look at each friendly unit standing adjacent to the fighter, but not behind it, that isn't in its own fight:

- it is **looking at the fight** → **+2**
- the fight is **off to its left or right** → **+1**
- it is looking away, or it is stuck in its own fight → **nothing**

Now take each unit's total and compare it against **the other unit's** three thresholds, left to right:

- reaches its **Retreat** → it gives ground
- reaches its **Reverse** → it is spun round
- reaches its **Rout** → it is removed, and its owner discards Morale cards

The results are carried out a stage at a time: both retreats, then both reverses, then both routs. In each stage the first player's blow lands first, so the second player's unit suffers first. A unit that is going to rout anyway can ignore its own retreat and reverse.

> **A worked fight.** _Your Legion (Attack 4) charges a Spearmen unit (Attack 3). The Spearmen are slower than your Legion (Speed 1 against 2), so they cannot back off and the two of you lock in. Two more of your Legions stand beside the fight: one is looking straight at it, one is side-on. That is +2 and +1, so your total is 4 + 3 = **7**. The Spearmen have nobody beside them: their total is **3**._
>
> _Spearmen thresholds are Retreat 6, Reverse 5, Rout 8 — a phalanx is easier to shake than to push back. Your 7 reaches Retreat and Reverse but not Rout — so they give ground, then spin round. Badly placed now._
>
> _Your Legion's thresholds are Retreat 5, Reverse 6, Rout 8. Their 3 reaches none of them. Your Legion is untouched._
>
> _The two supporting Legions did nothing but stand in the right place facing the right way, but they doubled the pressure._

### Phase 5 — Clean up

Put your face-up card onto your **played pile**. It is not in play any more.

Now each player, starting with the first player, decides whether to **rally**. Rallying is the only way to get cards back.

**If you rally:** shuffle your played pile well, draw a card, and **burn** it — it is gone from the game for good. Take everything else back into your hand: the rest of the played pile, and everything you discarded.

Then check that your cards can still hold your army together. Every card can steady a certain number of units, and says which kind. Add all that up and give each unit on the board one place. Any unit you cannot find a place for **routs**, and you pay its Morale.

**If you do not rally:** nothing happens. Your cards stay where they are and nobody checks your units. You just have fewer cards next round.

That is the central squeeze of the game. You need your cards back, and getting them back costs one card forever and puts your army to the test.

## 6. How to win

If any player's hand is ever empty at any point, no matter how briefly, that player loses the game; their army has lost its will to fight and flees the field. The other army has prevailed by keeping their fighting spirit and outlasting their foe.

## 7. Go and play

Start the next round. Play three or four before you worry about playing well.

When something comes up that this part does not answer — where exactly a retreating unit may go, whether a diagonal is blocked, how far a line can stretch — look in Part II under the obvious heading. Section 13 collects the questions that actually come up at the table, in the words people ask them.

# PART II — THE RULES

## 1. Golden rules

**1.1** Cards carry no rules text. Everything a card does is printed as values — an initiative number, a command, a round effect, a support grant, a commit stat — and this book governs how those values are read. A card never overrides a rule.

**1.2** If a rule says you _may_ do a thing, you may always decline. If it says you _must_, you must, and if you cannot, see **§11**.

**1.3** Where two effects would resolve at the same moment and the order matters, the **first player resolves first** (§4.1.5).

**1.4** All information on the board is open, except what a hidden stand conceals (§3.6). Hands are secret. A card played face down in Phase 1 is secret until revealed. Committed cards are revealed as they are committed.

**1.5** Nothing is remembered between rounds except what is physically on the table: positions, facings, card piles, whose initiative it is, and any engagement that was not settled. There is no tracking of damage, fatigue, or previous orders.

## 2. The battlefield

### 2.1 The board

**2.1.1** The board is a rectangular grid of square spaces, wider than it is deep. The two players face each other across it from the long edges. Depth is measured between the players; frontage runs across.

**2.1.2 No notation is needed.** Positions in this book are always described relative to a unit — the space ahead of it, the space behind it, its flanking spaces — and never by a fixed name. An unmarked mat is enough to play on. A digital board or a printed one may label its rows and columns as a convenience, but no rule depends on those labels and you never have to read one out.

**2.1.3** Three board sizes exist. Each is given as **depth × frontage**:

| Board    | Size    | Spaces | Used by        |
| -------- | ------- | ------ | -------------- |
| Small    | 8 × 12  | 96     | Tutorial, Mini |
| Standard | 12 × 18 | 216    | Standard       |
| Large    | 24 × 36 | 864    | Epic           |

**2.1.4** A space is open, level ground unless a terrain piece says otherwise (§2.5–2.8). Learn on a bare board.

### 2.2 Occupancy

**2.2.1** A space holds **at most one unit**, with one exception: two enemy units may occupy the same space while **engaged** (§6). Such a space is an **engaged space**.

**2.2.2** Commanders do not occupy space. A commander may share a space with a friendly unit, and more than one thing may stand in a space if one of them is a commander. A commander in a unit's space may move with that unit when it moves (§4.3.10), and always goes with it when it retreats (§9.1.8).<sup>[A4](#a4)</sup>

**2.2.3** No unit may enter or pass through an engaged space, for any reason, until that engagement is resolved. Neither may a commander.

### 2.3 Facing

**2.3.1** Every unit faces one of eight directions: toward any of the four sides of its space, or toward any of the four corners. Facing is open information (§1.4), and the whole game turns on it.

**2.3.2** Relative to a unit's facing, the eight surrounding spaces divide into three groups:

- **Front spaces** — three: the space it faces and the two to either side of that (45° off).
- **Flanking spaces** — two: directly to its left and right (90° off).
- **Back spaces** — three: the remaining spaces behind it.

```
@@FIG facing@@
        ↑  the way it is facing

     ·   ·   ·      ← its three front spaces
     ·  [U]  ·      ← its two flanking spaces
     ·   ·   ·      ← its three back spaces
```

**2.3.3 Spaces behind.** Beyond the three back spaces, the phrase _behind a unit_ means everything on the board on the far side of those three: the line through them, extended edge to edge, and every space beyond it. It is the whole back half of the field, not a narrow wedge. This matters for retreats (§9.1) and rear attacks (§6.2.2).

### 2.4 Adjacency, distance, and the forward arc

**2.4.1 Adjacent** means orthogonally or diagonally touching — up to eight spaces.

**2.4.2 Distance** is counted in steps where a diagonal step counts as **one**. "Within 3 spaces" means within three such steps. "Within 0 spaces" means the same space.

**2.4.3 The forward arc** is used for shooting (§7.4). Begin with the shooter's three front spaces; that is arc 1. For arc 2, add the three front spaces of each of those, and so on out to the shooter's Range. The result is a widening cone. The shooter's own space is not in its arc.

**2.4.4 Blocked diagonals.** Two enemy units standing in both of the orthogonal spaces that flank a diagonal **block** that diagonal. One enemy unit does not. A blocked diagonal cannot be stepped across (§5.4) and cannot carry support (§7.3.2).

Movement asks a **stricter question on top of this one**: a unit stepping across a diagonal must also be able to pass _through_ one of those two corner spaces, so a friend too stiff to pass through — and, later, terrain — can shut a diagonal for a mover (§5.4.5). That stricter test is the mover's alone. Support and commanders ask only the enemy question above; a wall of your own stiff troops never cuts off their reach.<sup>[A10](#a10)</sup>

```
@@FIG diagonal@@
   A diagonal step passes between the two
   spaces that flank the corner you cut.

   Enemies in BOTH of them:  blocked.
   An enemy in only one:     fine.
```

### 2.5 Terrain

Rough ground does not stop an army. It disorders it. A loose screen of skirmishers threads through scrub without breaking stride; a phalanx forty ranks deep comes out the other side as a crowd. The rules model that with one comparison, and the cost shows up everywhere the unit goes.

**2.5.1 Two numbers.** Every kind of terrain has a **terrain threshold**, how badly it disorders a formation, and a **speed limit**, how much it slows everyone whatever their order.<sup>[A3](#a3)</sup>

| Terrain                                      | Terrain threshold | Speed limit |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | ----------- |
| Road                                         | 0                 | none        |
| Open                                         | 1                 | none        |
| Fields (ploughed or irrigated, with ditches) | 2                 | 3           |
| Ford                                         | 2                 | 2           |
| Scrub                                        | 3                 | 3           |
| Stony                                        | 3                 | 3           |
| Mud                                          | 3                 | 2           |
| Light forest                                 | 4                 | 2           |
| Crags                                        | 4                 | 2           |
| Bank                                         | 4                 | 2           |
| Marsh                                        | 4                 | 1           |
| Dense forest                                 | 5                 | 1           |

**2.5.2 Deficit.** A unit whose current Flexibility is below the terrain threshold of its ground has a **deficit** equal to the difference. While it stands there it takes that deficit as a penalty to **every other stat**: Attack, all three of its combat thresholds, Speed and Range. Flexibility itself is not reduced — it is the measure, not the casualty.

> _A Manipular Legion (Flexibility 3) standing in light forest (terrain threshold 4) has a deficit of 1: it fights at Attack 3 instead of 4, and its thresholds drop from 5 / 6 / 8 to 4 / 5 / 7. Punic Citizen Spearmen (Flexibility 1) in the same wood have a deficit of 3: Attack 0, thresholds 3 / 2 / 5. Velites (Flexibility 5) have no deficit at all._

**2.5.3** Cards and commitments can lift a stat by 1 (§8). +1 Flexibility shrinks a deficit by one. That is the reason Flexibility is worth committing even when nobody is turning.

**2.5.4 Nothing is impassable except the board edge.** Every terrain can be crossed by someone; some units will need a lift to do it. A flank that cannot be turned is one that rests on the edge of the board.

**2.5.5 Roads.** A road has terrain threshold 0, so no unit has a deficit on it, even a unit whose card has dropped its Flexibility to 0. A rigid army can trade Flexibility for Speed and march on a road; the same trade on open ground costs it a deficit.

**2.5.6** Plain crops and stubble are open ground. Fields means ground with ditches and furrows a long rigid front cannot absorb. Stony ground is small loose stone; crags are large broken rock. Mud is the milder form of marsh. Terrains that share both numbers (stony and scrub) differ only in look.

### 2.6 Elevation

**2.6.1 Corners.** Each corner of a space is **high (+1)**, **level (0)** or **low (−1)**. Elevation belongs to corners, not whole spaces, because a melee happens inside one space and never across a border; a slope has to be able to run through the space where the fight is.<sup>[A16](#a16)</sup>

**2.6.2** No space has alternating corners (high, low, high, low round the square). A space split two and two runs high on one side and low on the other, and occurs only along a straight passage such as a valley or a ridge line.

**2.6.3 On the board.** Shade shows height: lighter for high, darker for low, graded where a space slopes. In a space that is not level, a faint triangle marks a high corner and a faint circle a low one. Unit bases are octagonal, so corners are never covered.

**2.6.4** Elevation does not slow a unit. It matters in combat (§7.6) and for line of sight (§7.4.6).

### 2.7 Rivers

**2.7.1 Water is low ground.** A wet corner is a low corner (−1). Wetness belongs to each space's own corner, so two neighbours can differ across the same point.

**2.7.2 Banks and fords.** Every space a river touches, on both sides, is **bank** or **ford** (§2.5.1). A river's depth shows in its mix: a shallow river is mostly ford, a deep one mostly bank. Crossing costs what entering those spaces costs; there is no separate crossing charge.

**2.7.3** A river running at 45° alternates spaces with one low corner on one side and three on the other. A space the river touches only at a point, on the outside of a bend, stays dry.

**2.7.4 Holding the bank.** A unit on the bank holds the high side of the slope down to the water (§7.6.1), but shares the bank's deficit. Standing one space back trades that height for time.

### 2.8 Terrain pieces

**2.8.1** Terrain comes on separate pieces, none more than five spaces across. Larger features come in several parts that join at marked points. Pieces never overlap and are never stacked.

**2.8.2** Everything about a space — its terrain, its corners, its water — is printed on the piece. There are no badges or numbers on the board: the art says what the ground is, and the scenario card lists each feature's terrain threshold and speed limit.

**2.8.3** Pieces are two-sided and asymmetrical, so each one offers choices when placed. Open-ground pieces exist too, for denying the enemy a place to put something worse (§3.4.6).

## 3. Armies and setup

### 3.1 Building an army

**3.1.1** An army is a list of units, a set of command cards and a few scenario cards (§3.1.8), built to the limits of the chosen game mode.

**3.1.2** The cards you choose **are** your hand. You hold all of them from the start, less any you discard in the bid for colour (§3.3.1). There is no pile to draw from, nothing in reserve, and no way to add a card once the battle has begun.

**3.1.3** Mode limits:

|                                      | Tutorial | Mini  | Standard | Epic  |
| ------------------------------------ | -------- | ----- | -------- | ----- |
| Board                                | Small    | Small | Standard | Large |
| Max total cost (units **and** cards) | no limit | 200   | 300      | 500   |
| Max different unit types             | 4        | 4     | 8        | 12    |
| **Minimum total Morale**             | none     | 10    | 20       | 30    |
| Cards of _each_ initiative value     | any      | 2     | 3        | 4     |
| Total cards in hand                  | any      | 8     | 12       | 16    |

Total cost adds up the cost of every unit you field, counting each copy, **and** the cost of every command card and scenario card you bring. Total Morale adds up the Morale of every unit you field, counting each copy.

**3.1.4** Each unit type shows a **limit** — the most copies of it one army may field (never more than 12).

**3.1.5** No unit type may be listed twice, and no command card may appear twice in one army.

**3.1.6 The morale floor is a leash.** You must field at least the minimum total Morale, which means you cannot build an army of cheap, worthless troops that costs you nothing when it dies. Somebody in your list has to matter.

**3.1.7 The initiative spread is also a leash.** You must take an equal number of cards at each initiative value 1 through 4. No army can be built to always go first.

**3.1.8 Scenario cards.** Your list also carries **three scenario cards** (§3.2), bought from the same points as your units and command cards. Scenario cards are not command cards: they never enter your hand.<sup>[A5](#a5)</sup>

### 3.2 Scenarios

Most battles are fought from a scenario. Where you fight matters as much as how you fight and with which troops, and both players buy a say in it before the first unit is placed.

**3.2.1** A scenario card shows a deployment zone for Black and one for White, a set of **primary** terrain features, used if this scenario is the one played, and a set of **secondary** features, used when White picks the card to supply terrain for someone else's scenario (§3.3.5). A feature may be a piece of open ground.

**3.2.3** Scenario cards are **public**. Both lists' cards are on the table before the bid, so each player bids knowing what the other could choose.

**3.2.2** Weather belongs to the day, so features that depend on it, such as mud, appear only as primary terrain. Secondary terrain is standing landform.

### 3.3 The setup sequence

Setup is a short series of decisions, each made once, in this order. The sequence is settled in outline; its details are still being playtested.<sup>[A5](#a5)</sup>

**3.3.1 Bid.** The two players bid for the choice of colour, with command cards.

- The player whose army costs more bids first. Bidding first reveals intent, which compensates the smaller army. With equal costs, decide at random.
- The first bidder may open, or may cede the colour choice to the opponent outright.
- A bid is the **total number of command cards you have discarded**, face down. Each raise must beat your opponent's total.
- You may never bid your hand down below **two cards**, since an empty hand loses (§11.1).
- Holding exactly two cards, before raising you may **burn** one of your discarded cards at random and take the rest back into your hand, then keep discarding. Cards taken back still count toward your bid, so a burn lets a bid run past the size of your hand.
- Either player may pass at any time, conceding.
- Discarded bid cards come back at your first rally (§10.3), like any discard. A burn here is not a rally and triggers no support check, but the card is gone, and whatever units it held together will be exposed at your next one (§10.4).

**3.3.2 Colour.** The winner of the bid chooses to be White or Black. **White holds initiative** going into round one; Black chooses the battlefield and deploys first.

**3.3.3 Veto.** White vetoes one of Black's scenario cards.

**3.3.4 Scenario.** Black chooses the scenario from its remaining cards. That sets both deployment zones and the primary terrain.

**3.3.5 White's terrain.** Knowing Black's choice, White chooses which of its own scenario cards supplies the secondary terrain.

**3.3.6 Placement** (§3.4).

**3.3.7 Deployment** (§3.5–3.6).

### 3.4 Placing terrain

**3.4.1** The players take turns placing features, **White first**. Black places the primary features of the chosen scenario; White places the secondary features of its own card. Place them in any order. You need not place them all.

**3.4.2** A feature is one or more pieces placed together. Features may touch the board edge but never each other, not even at a corner.

**3.4.3** A feature made of several parts joins only at its marked points, in its printed shape. You may place a part on its own only if its join point lies on the board edge, so the rest of the feature runs off the board.

**3.4.4** A feature that cannot be placed anywhere is lost.

**3.4.5 Passing.** Passing ends your placement for good. When one player passes or runs out, the other places the rest of their features one after another, as far as they fit.

**3.4.6** The ground no one places is open. A player who passes gives up control of where that open ground lies; an open-ground piece is how you buy it back. Large features claim the most space but may not fit if left late, so the order you place in is a real decision.

### 3.5 Deployment

**3.5.1** **Black deploys first**, then White. Each player places their whole army at once, every unit in their own zone, one per space, each facing any direction they choose. White deploys last, seeing the whole board except what stands conceal (§3.6).<sup>[A5](#a5)</sup>

**3.5.2** Units may deploy into terrain, but not where they would stand at a deficit (§2.5.2) unless every other space in the zone is already taken.

**3.5.3** Place your commander on any space in your zone. It may share that space with one of your own units.

**3.5.4 Without a scenario.** On a bare board, each deployment zone is the band of **three rows nearest your own edge**, less the **outermost two columns at each end**:

| Board    | Zone        | Frontage       |
| -------- | ----------- | -------------- |
| Small    | 3 rows deep | 8 spaces wide  |
| Standard | 3 rows deep | 14 spaces wide |
| Large    | 3 rows deep | 32 spaces wide |

The two columns at each end lie outside both zones. They are the open flanks: nobody begins there, and getting into them is most of what fast troops are for. Choose colours any way you like. With no battlefield to choose, the trade is simpler: **White deploys first and holds initiative**, so whoever shows their hand first also moves first. Black deploys seeing White's whole army.<sup>[A5](#a5)</sup>

### 3.6 Hidden units

An army in a wood or behind a ridge cannot be seen from across the field. A **stand** marks a place where something might be waiting.<sup>[A17](#a17)</sup>

**3.6.1 Components.** Each colour has a matched set of numbered stands and numbered cards.

**3.6.2 Where.** At deployment, a space that is **hidden** and legal for you to deploy into may take a stand instead of a unit. A space is hidden if no line of sight (§7.4.6) reaches it. Black, deploying first, judges this against White's deployment zone. White, deploying last, judges it against Black's actual deployment, stands included.

**3.6.3 Decoys.** The unit a stand represents is placed face down on the card with the stand's number. A stand may also be a **decoy**, with no unit at all. Not knowing which is the point.

**3.6.4** A hidden unit cannot move. Every stand counts as an enemy unit for movement, for both players: nothing passes through one.

**3.6.5 Reveal.** A stand is revealed when an opposing unit gains line of sight to it or becomes adjacent to it. Its owner may also reveal it at the end of Phase 2. The owner chooses its facing as it is revealed, and from then on it is an ordinary unit.

**3.6.6 Moves are not interrupted.** A unit completes its move before anything is revealed; every stand it can then see or touch is revealed at the end of the move. A unit that wants to look before committing itself should stop short.

**3.6.7 Moving into a stand.** A unit that moves directly into a stand's space reveals it. A real unit there turns to face the mover, and the two resolve a front engagement (§6.3.2), whichever side the mover came from. Here §6.2 and §6.3.1 do not apply. A decoy is removed, and the move ends there.

**3.6.8** Stands can only go in hidden spaces inside your own zone, which in practice means forests and the far side of hills. Most scenarios offer few such spaces, and terrain, not a rule, is what limits them.

## 4. The round

A battle is fought in rounds. Each round has five phases, resolved in order, and no phase may be skipped — though a phase with nothing in it ends immediately.

### 4.1 Phase 1 — Play Cards

**4.1.1** Both players **simultaneously and secretly** choose one card from hand and place it face down.

**4.1.2** Reveal both cards together. Each is now **in play** and stays face up in front of its owner for the whole round.

**4.1.3** Compare initiative values. **The lower value takes initiative.**

**4.1.4** On a **tie**, initiative stays with whoever already had it.

**4.1.5** The player with initiative is the **first player** for the rest of the round and acts first in every phase that has an order. The other is the **second player**.

**4.1.6** From this moment until Cleanup your revealed card is doing two jobs: it grants its Command (§4.3) and it imposes its Round Effect (§4.3.7). It is no longer in your hand, so it cannot be committed (§8.6). Its unit support is unaffected — support is only ever measured after a rally, when every card you still own is back in your hand (§10.4).

**⚠ Edge case.** A card in play cannot be committed (§8). Once revealed, it is spoken for.

### 4.2 Phase 2 — Move Commanders

**4.2.1** The first player moves their commander, then the second player moves theirs.

**4.2.2** A commander may move up to **4 spaces**. Diagonal steps count as one.

**4.2.3** A commander has no facing and may change direction freely mid-move.

**4.2.4** A commander may enter and pass through any space **not containing an enemy unit** — including empty spaces and spaces holding friendly units. It may not enter a space holding an enemy unit, and therefore may not enter an engaged space.

**4.2.5** A commander may not cross a diagonal blocked by enemies (§2.4.4).

**4.2.6** A commander may stay where it is.

**4.2.7 Why this matters.** A card's command and its round effect each may carry an **inspiration range**, measured from your commander. A range printed as **—** means no restriction. A range of **0** means the same space as your commander. A round effect that applies to **all** of a trait reaches every unit of that trait, wherever it stands. Where a range is printed, your general's position decides which of your troops it reaches.<sup>[A6](#a6)</sup>

**4.2.8 Commander defeat.** A commander is defeated if it stands in a space where **one of its own units** routs in combat. An enemy routing at its feet does not touch it, and a unit that routs at the support check (§10.4) does not defeat it either.<sup>[A15](#a15)</sup> Defeat costs nothing on its face: no cards, no Morale. The commander is simply gone — and with it, every inspiration range on your cards reaches nothing. Roughly half a hand leans on that reach, and some cards go dead outright. The blow is structural, not immediate.

### 4.3 Phase 3 — Issue Commands

Your card says what you are allowed to order this round and how much of it. It might be _four units_, or it might be _two lines_ — a line being a whole rank of troops moving as one. You spend that allowance on the units you want to act, then you carry the orders out one by one.

The allowance is the interesting part. A card that orders one line can move eight units if they happen to be dressed in a rank; if your army is scattered, the same card moves one, since a lone unit is a line of one. Formation is not decoration here — it is how much you get to do.

This phase has four steps: the first player **issues** all their commands, then **resolves** all of them; then the second player does the same.

#### Issuing

**4.3.1** Your in-play card shows exactly one Command, with a **type** (Move or Shoot), a **size** and a **number**.

**4.3.2 Size: units.** You make **one** order, selecting **between one and the printed number** of eligible units. The number is a ceiling, not a quota. You may instead decline to order at all.

**4.3.3 Size: lines.** You make that many **separate** orders, each one aimed at a single **line** (§4.3.5). Two lines means two orders, resolved independently. You may stop after fewer. Whatever you do not issue is forfeited, and if none of your remaining orders can be given to anyone, you must stop.

**4.3.4 Eligibility.** A unit may be ordered only if it satisfies _all_ of the Command's restrictions, and has not already been ordered this round (§4.3.6):

- **Trait restrictions** — the unit must have **every** listed trait.
- **Unit restrictions** — the unit must be one of the listed types.
- **Inspiration range** — where one is printed, the unit must be within that many spaces of **your own commander** (§2.4.2), measured when you issue the order.

An **engaged** unit cannot be ordered. It is locked in its fight until the engagement ends (§6.1.2).<sup>[A1](#a1)</sup>

**4.3.5 Lines.** A **line** is a rank of your own units standing shoulder to shoulder:

- Every unit lies in the **flanking space** of the one beside it — that is, the line runs _across_ their facing, not along it.
- Every unit faces the **same direction, or exactly the opposite** direction as the unit you name first. A rank that has about-faced is still a rank.
- The line is **unbroken**: no gaps, no enemies, no non-qualifying units in the middle.
- A line is **1 to 8 units** long.

You select a line by naming its two ends. The **near end you name must satisfy the full restrictions** including inspiration range; the **rest of the line need only satisfy the trait and type restrictions**. A line therefore reaches beyond your general's voice, so long as it starts within it.

If an unbroken rank is longer than eight units, you choose which eight.<sup>[A7](#a7)</sup>

**An engaged unit breaks the line.** It cannot be commanded (§4.3.4), and a line is not allowed to run past it: commanding a line means every unit in it is commanded, with no silent passengers. Its space holds an enemy besides, which the unbroken test already refuses. A looser rule — the line flowing past its engaged members — may come back if play-tests miss it; until then the simple rule stands.<sup>[A1](#a1)</sup>

**4.3.6 One order per unit.** A unit may be commanded only **once per round**, whether as a single unit or as part of a line. Already-commanded units cannot be included in a later line, and a line cannot be drawn _through_ one — it stops there.

**4.3.7 Round Effects are not orders.** A card's Round Effect applies to every one of your units that meets its restrictions, all round, commanded or not. Its restrictions are read separately from the Command's and are usually different — in particular, a round effect's inspiration range is checked again whenever its bonus matters, wherever the unit is standing at that moment, not only when orders are given. For a move, that moment is the moment the move begins, once for the whole move (§5.1.4).<sup>[A6](#a6)</sup>

**4.3.8 Command modifiers apply only to the commanded.** A Command's printed modifiers (e.g. _+1 Attack, −2 Speed_) apply for the whole round, but **only to units that actually received that order**. They stay in force through Phase 4 — a unit ordered to charge still has its bonus when the melee resolves.

#### Resolving

**4.3.9** Resolve your issued commands one at a time, in any order you like, finishing each unit before beginning the next.

**4.3.10 Resolving a movement order** (§5):

1. Choose a unit that has not yet acted.
2. You may **commit** a card for **+1 Speed** or **+1 Flexibility** (§8). Optional.<sup>[A8](#a8)</sup>
3. Move the unit (§5).
4. If it moved into an enemy-occupied space, an **engagement** begins and is set up now (§6).

A commander standing in the unit's space may move with it, and ends in the unit's final space. It may stay behind instead. Otherwise your commander moves only in Phase 2.<sup>[A9](#a9)</sup>

A commander may ride along even when the unit moves **into an engagement**. This is the one way a commander ends in a space holding an enemy: the commander is committed to the fight, and falls with its unit if that unit routs (§4.2.8) — the enemy's rout does not touch it. A huge risk, bought for a general standing at the point of decision.<sup>[A9](#a9)</sup>

**4.3.11 Resolving a ranged attack order** (§7.4):

1. Choose the shooter. It must be unengaged, have Range of at least 1, and have a legal target at its own Range. A unit with no such target is skipped: a Range commitment can carry a shot further, but it does not give a unit a shot it did not have (author, 2026-11-09: kept simple for now).
2. Before choosing a target, the **attacker** may commit one card. This is the attacker's only commitment for the shot, and it applies to the whole attack: +1 **Range** extends the forward arc one space further out (§2.4.3), +1 **Attack** adds to the blow, and a card naming both gives both (§8). Optional.<sup>[B6](#b6)</sup>
3. Choose one enemy target within its forward arc (§2.4.3), counting any Range committed. You cannot shoot into a melee: a unit in an engaged space is never a target.
4. Optionally name **supporters** — other units you ordered with this same command that have not yet shot, and that could each independently shoot that same target at their own Range; the attacker's card does not reach them. Each supporter adds **+1**, and naming it spends that unit's own shot (§7.3.4).
5. The **defender** may then commit a card for **+1 Defence**, or for **+1 Flexibility** to shrink a deficit on its thresholds (§2.5.2, §8.5). Optional.<sup>[B1](#b1)</sup>
6. Resolve the attack (§7).

A card naming Flexibility helps a shooter standing at a deficit: +1 Flexibility shrinks the deficit, and with it the penalty to Range and Attack (§2.5.2).

**⚠ Edge case.** An engaged unit can neither shoot nor support a shot. It is busy.

**⚠ Edge case.** Shooting is never automatic. Units with Range do nothing at all unless a Shoot command orders them to shoot.

**⚠ Edge case.** The target must also be in **line of sight** (§7.4.6). Units never block sight: you may always shoot over your own line, or over a melee.

### 4.4 Phase 4 — Resolve Melee

**4.4.1** Every engaged space on the board fights this phase. There is no choice about _whether_.

**4.4.2** The **first player** chooses which engagement to resolve, resolves it completely, then chooses the next, until none remain. The choice of order is entirely theirs.

**4.4.3** Resolve each engagement per §7.2.

**4.4.4** If there are no engagements, the phase ends immediately.

### 4.5 Phase 5 — Cleanup

**4.5.1 Discard played cards.** Both players move their in-play card to their **played pile**. It is not in your hand and it is not in your discard; it sits in the played pile until you rally or the game ends.

**4.5.2 The first player decides whether to rally**, and fully resolves that decision (§10.3) including the support check and any routs it causes.

**4.5.3 Then the second player** does the same.

**4.5.4** The round ends. A new round begins at Phase 1.

## 5. Movement

A unit walks forwards. That is the only thing it can do. Everything else about movement follows from that one restriction: to go anywhere other than straight ahead, a unit must first turn to face the way it wants to go, and turning is a separate, limited resource from walking.

A move is therefore a short sequence of steps and turns, in whatever order you like, paid for out of two allowances that refresh each time the unit is ordered.

### 5.1 The two resources

**5.1.1** A move spends two things: **Speed**, for stepping, and **Flexibility**, for turning. Both refresh every time the unit is ordered — they are not a pool that depletes over the battle. Both are the unit's **current** values: printed value plus any round effect that reaches it and any modifier from the command it was given, and any card committed for it (§4.3.7, §4.3.8, §8).

**5.1.2** Each step into an adjacent space costs **1 Speed**. Diagonal steps cost 1, same as orthogonal.

**5.1.3** A unit only ever steps **in the direction it is currently facing**. To go somewhere else, turn first.

**5.1.4 Sampled where the move begins.** A unit's Speed and Flexibility for a move are read **once, where and when its own move begins** — commit window included — and never again along the way. A round effect's range is measured against the starting space: a unit that sets off inside its general's reach keeps the bonus for the whole move, even if its first step leaves; one that merely passes through the range on its way gets nothing. Everything in this game is read at the moment it binds: a command when it is issued (§4.3.7), a move when it begins, a blow where the fighter stands when it lands (§7), and the ground wherever the unit actually treads (§2.5, §5.5).

### 5.2 Turning

**5.2.1** A facing change costs **1 Flexibility** and turns the unit either **45° to one side** or **180° about**. Wheeling 90° takes two turns, and so two Flexibility.<sup>[A2](#a2)</sup>

**5.2.2** Turns and steps may come in **any order**, and a unit may turn several times in a row. The only limit is the Speed and Flexibility it has to spend.

**5.2.3** A unit with Flexibility 0 cannot turn at all. It moves in a straight line or not at all.

### 5.3 What you may move into

A unit may **end its move** in a space that is:

- **its own starting space** (it did not move, or it turned on the spot), or
- **empty**, or
- **occupied by a single enemy unit**, if it may legally engage it (§6.2).

A unit may **never** end its move in a space occupied by another friendly unit, nor in an engaged space.

### 5.4 What you may move through

**5.4.1** Empty spaces: freely.

**5.4.2** Spaces with a **friendly** unit: only if **your Flexibility plus theirs is 6 or more**, using current values including all modifiers. Two stiff formations cannot interpenetrate; nimble troops can filter through.

**5.4.3** Spaces with an **enemy** unit: never.

**5.4.4** **Engaged spaces**: never.

**5.4.5 Diagonal steps** additionally require that at least **one** of the two orthogonal spaces flanking the diagonal be one you could pass through under 5.4.1 to 5.4.4. You cannot slip between two units standing corner to corner, and you cannot cross a diagonal blocked by enemies (§2.4.4). The step still costs only 1 Speed. The test looks only at those two corner spaces, never at the space you step into. A diagonal step that ends on a single enemy unit is an engagement, and it follows this same test and the engagement rules (§6.2) with no special case for being diagonal: enemies in both corner spaces stop you, an enemy in only one does not.<sup>[A10](#a10)</sup><sup>[A12](#a12)</sup>

**⚠ Edge case.** If you pass through several friendly units in one move, check the combined-Flexibility test **separately for each one**.

### 5.5 Terrain and movement

**5.5.1 The worst ground counts.** Look at every space the move touches: where it starts, every space it passes through, and where it ends. The **highest terrain threshold** and the **lowest speed limit** among them both count, even if they come from different spaces.<sup>[A3](#a3)</sup>

**5.5.2 Work out the move's Speed in this order:**

1. Cap the unit's printed Speed at that lowest speed limit.
2. Subtract the deficit from that highest terrain threshold (§2.5.2), but not below 0.
3. Add modifiers: round effect, command and commitment.

The result is the unit's Speed for this move, and only this move. After it, the unit's deficit comes only from the space it stands in. A step the move's Speed does not cover cannot be taken.

Because modifiers come last, a speed bonus can move a unit the deficit had stopped, and can carry a unit past the speed limit.

> _Light horse (Speed 5, Flexibility 3) entering light forest (threshold 4, limit 2): cap at 2, deficit 1, Speed 1. Chariots (4, 1) in fields (2, 3): cap at 3, deficit 1, Speed 2. A pike phalanx (1, 1) cannot enter fields at all — cap 1, deficit 1, Speed 0 — unless a card or commitment lifts its Speed or its Flexibility._

| Unit (Speed, Flexibility) | Fields | Scrub | Light forest, bank | Dense forest |
| ------------------------- | ------ | ----- | ------------------ | ------------ |
| Light horse (5, 3)        | 3      | 3     | 1                  | —            |
| Close cavalry (4, 2)      | 3      | 2     | —                  | —            |
| Chariots (4, 1)           | 2      | 1     | —                  | —            |
| Phalanx (1, 1)            | —      | —     | —                  | —            |

A dash means the unit cannot enter without a lift.

**5.5.3 Diagonal steps** count as passing through one of the two orthogonal spaces they cut between, your choice, and pay that space's terrain. A diagonal never crosses a river for free, just as it never slips between two enemies (§5.4.5).

**5.5.4 No exceptions by unit type.** Cavalry, elephants and chariots follow the same rule as everyone else. Their low Flexibility does the work.

**⚠ Edge case.** A unit may be ordered and then not move at all — a legal move of zero steps and zero turns. It still counts as commanded, and still receives the Command's modifiers (§4.3.8). Ordering a unit to stand still and gain +1 Attack is a real and often correct play.

## 6. Engagements

Units do not stand next to each other and trade blows across a boundary. To fight, one unit walks into the other's space, and the two of them share it until the matter is settled. That shared space is an engagement.

Two questions follow, and the rest of this section answers them. _May I walk in at all?_ — which depends on the side I approach from. _And what kind of fight is it?_ — which depends on which way the two of us end up facing. Coming at a unit from behind does not merely give you an advantage in the fight; it means there is no fight.

### 6.1 What an engagement is

**6.1.1** An **engagement** is created the moment a unit ends a step in a space occupied by a single enemy unit. Both units now stand in that one space. It is an **engaged space**.<sup>[A11](#a11)</sup>

**6.1.2** Engagements are created in Phase 3 and fought in Phase 4. Between those moments the two units are locked together: neither can move, neither can shoot, neither can support anything, and nothing may enter or cross their space. A fight that neither side wins outright goes on into the next round (§7.2.3); the lock holds until it ends.

**6.1.3** The unit that moved in is the **engaging unit**. The unit that was already there is the **defender**.

### 6.2 When you may engage

Look at which of the defender's spaces you are stepping **from**:

**6.2.1 From a flanking space:** always allowed, whether the last step is straight or diagonal. A diagonal last step passes the same corner test as any other diagonal step (§5.4.5).<sup>[A12](#a12)</sup>

**6.2.2 From a back space:** allowed **only if your unit began this entire move in the region behind the defender** (§2.3.3). You cannot loop around a formation's flank and take it in the back in the same order. You must already have been behind it.

**6.2.3 From a front space:** allowed only if your unit **ends its move facing directly opposite the defender** — squared up, head on. If it is not already facing that way, it spends 1 Flexibility to turn as it closes, and without that Flexibility it cannot engage from there.<sup>[A13](#a13)</sup>

### 6.3 What kind of engagement it is

**6.3.1** Compare the **engaging unit's final facing** with the **defender's facing**. Note that this is facing against facing, not position.

| Engaging unit's facing, relative to defender's | Type      |
| ---------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| The same, or 45° off either way                | **Rear**  |
| 90° off either way                             | **Flank** |
| Directly opposite                              | **Front** |

Read it as a picture: if the two units are looking at each other, that is a Front engagement. If one is looking across the other, that is a Flank. If they are both looking the same way, the attacker has come from behind. Because a front approach always squares up (§6.2.3), no engagement falls between these three.<sup>[A13](#a13)</sup>

**6.3.2 Front engagement.** The defender may be offered the chance to fall back:

1. The defender may **commit** a card for +1 Speed or +1 Flexibility (§8). Optional. (The engaging player had their chance before moving, §4.3.10.)
2. Compare current **Speed**: each unit's full Speed, including round effects, command modifiers and the deficit of the ground it stands on (§2.5.2), not what is left of its move. Ground counts here as it does everywhere. Each unit's Speed is the one its own move runs on (§5.1.4): the engaging unit's was sampled where its move began, commitment and all (§4.3.10); the defender samples here and now, where it stands with its commit window open — the start of the retreat it is weighing. The defender may retreat **only if its Speed is strictly greater** than the engaging unit's.<sup>[A11](#a11)</sup>
3. If it may and chooses to, it makes a retreat move (§9.1). The engagement ends at once; no melee is fought.
4. Otherwise both units stay locked and fight in Phase 4.

**6.3.3 Rear engagement.** The defender is **routed immediately** (§9.3). No melee, no commitment, no reply. Its owner pays its Morale at once. The engaging unit remains where it stands.<sup>[B10](#b10)</sup>

**6.3.4 Flank engagement.** The defender is **forced to rotate** to face the engaging unit — its new facing is directly opposite the engaging unit's. It may not retreat. The two fight in Phase 4.

A flank attack does no damage by itself. What it does is remove the defender's options: it cannot fall back, and it enters the melee having just been turned, which usually costs it the support of the units beside it (§7.3.1).

**⚠ Edge case.** A defender that is routed by a rear engagement pays Morale immediately, in Phase 3 — not in Phase 4, and not at Cleanup.

**⚠ Edge case.** If several of your units engage in one phase, each engagement is fully set up as its movement resolves. A unit that has already been engaged by one of your units cannot be engaged again — its space is now an engaged space and nothing may enter it (§2.2.3). You get one attacker per defender.

## 7. Combat

All combat, melee or ranged, works the same way: build an attack value, compare it to the target's three thresholds, apply what it reaches.

### 7.1 Attack value

**7.1.1** Start with the unit's printed **Attack**.

**7.1.2** Add every modifier that applies. They are simply summed, so the order does not matter:

1. **Round Effect** of the attacker's own in-play card, if the unit meets its restrictions (§4.3.7).
2. **Command modifiers** of that same card, if the unit was commanded this round (§4.3.8).
3. **Committed card**, if one was committed for this combat (§8).
4. **Ground**: minus the unit's deficit (§2.5.2), plus or minus elevation (§7.6).

**7.1.3** Then add **support** (§7.3).

**7.1.4** The result is the attack value. Modifiers may be negative, but **no stat in this game goes below zero** — the attack value included. The floor is applied once, to the finished total: a sum that lands below zero counts as zero. It is never applied mid-sum, so a stat cannot hit the floor early and have a later bonus rebound it above what the raw arithmetic gives.<sup>[B2](#b2)</sup>

**7.1.5** A deficit also lowers all three of the unit's **thresholds** while it stands on that ground (§2.5.2). The same three card sources can change a unit's **thresholds**. A Round Effect or Command modifier that names Defence raises Retreat, Reverse and Rout together, for as long as the card is in play. The same inspiration range and unit restrictions apply as for any Round Effect.

### 7.2 Melee

**7.2.1** Melee is **mutual and simultaneous**. Both units attack. Both may break. There is no attacker and defender in a melee, only two units in the same space.

**7.2.2** Procedure for one engagement:

1. The **first player** may commit a card for their unit. Optional.
2. The **second player** may then do the same. Optional.
3. Compute both attack values (§7.1), each including that side's own melee support. Support is counted on the board as it stands now, before either unit is touched.
4. Compare each unit's attack value against the **other** unit's thresholds.<sup>[B3](#b3)</sup>
5. Apply the results a stage at a time: both retreats, then both reverses, then both routs. In each stage the first player's blow lands first, so the **second player's unit suffers first** (§7.5.6).

A card naming Flexibility helps a unit fighting at a deficit: +1 Flexibility shrinks the deficit, and with it the penalty to Attack and thresholds (§2.5.2).

**7.2.3** If neither unit routs or retreats, they remain engaged and will fight again next round.

**7.2.4** In Phase 4 the first player chooses which engagement to fight next, until none are left (§4.4).

> _Attacker: Attack 4, +1 from its card's Round Effect, +3 support = **8**._
> _Defender: Attack 4, +2 support = **6**._
> _Attacker's thresholds — Retreat 5, Reverse 6, Rout 8. Defender's — Retreat 5, Reverse 6, Rout 7._
> _8 meets the defender's Rout: the defender breaks._
> _6 meets the attacker's Retreat and Reverse but not its Rout: the attacker falls back, then turns about._

> _A real pairing. A **Manipular Legion** (Attack 4; thresholds 5 / 6 / 8) with +3 support strikes **Hellenistic Hoplites** (Attack 3; thresholds 7 / 6 / 8) with +2 support. The Legion's value is 7, the Hoplites' is 5._
> _7 meets the Hoplites' Retreat (7) and Reverse (6), but not Rout (8): they fall back and turn about. 5 meets the Legion's Retreat (5) but not its Reverse (6): it falls back and stays facing the enemy. Nobody is removed, and nobody pays Morale._

### 7.3 Support

A unit does not fight alone. The friends standing around it add to its blow — and how much they add depends on whether they are actually facing the fight or looking somewhere else.

Support is typically +1 or +2, about the size of any other modifier. A unit wrapped on several sides can gather far more, but a full envelopment like that is rarely feasible. It is also a number you have a lot of control over, because it is decided entirely by where your units stand and which way you have pointed them. Melee and ranged support work differently and are described separately below.

(Do not confuse this with **unit support**, the cards-hold-units rule of §10.4. They share a word and nothing else.)

#### 7.3.1 Melee support

A friendly unit backs up your fighting unit in melee when it is in a position to lend a hand. All of these must be true:

- it stands **right beside** the fighter, diagonals included;
- it is **not behind** the fighter, meaning not in one of the fighter's back spaces;
- it is **free**: a unit locked in a fight of its own lends nothing;
- if it stands on a diagonal, the way between the two is **open** (§2.4.4). It is shut only when **both** of the two spaces at the corners between them hold enemy units. One enemy there does not shut it.

How much it lends depends on **where it is looking**, which is to say where the fighter lies from the supporter's point of view:

| The fighter is in the supporter's…  | Support |
| ----------------------------------- | ------- |
| **front spaces** (looking at it)    | **+2**  |
| **flanking spaces** (off to a side) | **+1**  |
| **back spaces** (looking away)      | none    |

**7.3.2** Add up every qualifying supporter. There is no cap, but the total stays modest in practice. One or two supporters, +1 or +2, is typical. +4, two strong supporters, is a total envelopment.

```
@@FIG support@@
      [S1]  [S2]
         \  /
         [ U ]  ──▶ engaged here

   S1 faces toward U      → +2
   S2 faces across U      → +1
   U fights at Attack + 3
```

> _Check the geometry before counting. U faces North, so its back spaces are the three behind it. S1 stands diagonally ahead of U on one side, turned toward it: +2. S2 stands diagonally ahead on the other side, turned so that U lies off its shoulder: +1. A third friend directly behind U adds nothing, however it faces._

**7.3.3** Support is recalculated fresh for each combat, from the board as it stands at that moment. Units that routed earlier in the phase are simply not there any more. This is why the first player's choice of which fight to resolve first matters (§4.4.2): clearing a unit away can change the support in every fight after it.

#### 7.3.4 Ranged support

Ranged support is much simpler and quite different from melee support. Each supporting unit adds **+1**, flat, regardless of facing or position.

A supporter must be one of your units that

- was **ordered by this same Shoot command**,
- has **not yet taken** its own shot this phase, and
- could **independently make that same shot** — unengaged, Range 1 or more, the target within its own forward arc.

Naming a unit as a supporter **spends its shot**. A command that orders four archers may therefore shoot as four separate attacks, or as a single attack at +3, or anything in between. Concentrating shots is how you break a hard target; spreading it is how you cover a frontage.

### 7.4 Ranged attacks

**7.4.1** The shooter must be **unengaged** and have current **Range** of at least 1.

**7.4.2** The target must be an **unengaged** enemy unit in the shooter's **forward arc** (§2.4.3), out to its current Range, and in **line of sight** (§7.4.6). A unit locked in an engagement cannot be shot, though it can be shot over.

**7.4.3** Attack value = the shooter's Attack (with its modifiers, §7.1) + 1 per supporter (§7.3.4).

**7.4.4** A shot is **one way**. The target does not shoot back.

**7.4.5** The attacker commits first, once, before choosing the target; the card counts for the whole attack (Range for the arc, Attack for the blow). Then, with the target named, the defender commits for Defence, raising the target's thresholds against this shot (§8.5).<sup>[B1](#b1)</sup>

**7.4.6 Line of sight.** A shooter can see a target if a straight line can be drawn from **any corner of the shooter's space** to **any corner of the target's space** without touching a blocking space.<sup>[A18](#a18)</sup>

- **What blocks:** light forest, dense forest, and any space higher than both ends of the line (compare the sum of each space's four corners).
- **Any contact blocks.** A line that touches a blocking space anywhere, along an edge or at a single corner, is blocked. Touching it at the line's own end points does not count.
- **The target's own space always counts.** So a unit can see out of a forest, but cannot be seen in one, until the shooter is adjacent.
- **Adjacent spaces** always have line of sight.
- **Units never block sight**, friend or enemy. Distance is the cost of a shot, not the bodies in between.

> _Skirmishers at the edge of a wood can shoot out at anyone in their arc. Nobody can shoot back at them unless they come right up to the trees._

### 7.5 Applying the result

**7.5.1** Compare the attack value against each of the target's three thresholds. A threshold is met if the attack value is **equal to or greater than** it. The three are printed on the unit in the order they resolve, left to right:

| Threshold met | Result                                                                                    |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Retreat**   | The unit falls back (§9.1).                                                               |
| **Reverse**   | The unit is turned 180° on the spot (§9.2).                                               |
| **Rout**      | The unit is removed from the battle; its owner discards cards equal to its Morale (§9.3). |

No unit prints a threshold of 0.

**7.5.2** An attack will often meet more than one threshold. A unit's results come in this order: **retreat, then reverse, then rout.** In a melee the two units' results are interleaved (§7.5.6).

**7.5.3 A rout saves you the trouble.** If the attack reaches the Rout threshold, the unit is leaving the board, so whether it would also have fallen back or turned about changes nothing for that unit. It may ignore its own retreat and reverse. Its rout still comes up in its place in the order (§7.5.6).

**7.5.4** A unit that retreats and reverses does both, in that order: it falls back, then turns about. Falling back first is often what makes the reverse possible at all — see 7.5.5.

**7.5.5** **A unit that is still sharing a space with an enemy does not reverse.** The reverse is checked at the moment it comes due. If the unit is still engaged then, it simply stays as it is. Within an engagement the two units are in the same situation and are suffering similar fatigue, so neither is yet worse off than the other. What the blow did to a unit shows on the battlefield once the engagement is resolved. A unit whose enemy has since fallen back or been removed is no longer engaged, so it does reverse, and that includes a unit that has just driven its enemy back.

**7.5.6** In a melee, both units' results are decided from the same two attack values, then **carried out a stage at a time: both retreats, then both reverses, then both routs.** In each stage the **first player's blow lands first**, so the unit it strikes, the second player's, suffers first. The full order is second player's unit retreats, first player's unit retreats, second player's reverses, first player's reverses, second player's routs, first player's routs. Alternating at every step is how initiative counts in a melee: the first player strikes first, and the second player's unit takes the result first at each one. This matters in three places. A unit that has already fallen back is no longer there to keep the other engaged, so the other can reverse (§7.5.5). A unit that is going to rout is still on the board until the rout stage, so the unit engaged with it is still engaged at the reverse stage and does not reverse. And if the second player's Morale payment is more than its owner can bear, the game ends there (§11.2), before the first player's unit's rout is carried out. A unit that must retreat and cannot (§9.1.6) routs **in the retreat stage**, at its own retreat's turn, not later with the routs: the rout takes the place of the retreat it could not make.<sup>[B9](#b9)</sup>

**⚠ Edge case.** A unit that **must** retreat but **has no legal retreat** (§9.1.5) **routs instead** — the one case where a result early in the order escalates into the last one. It is among the most dangerous situations in the game, and the reason a unit's back should never be against something it cannot cross.<sup>[B4](#b4)</sup>

### 7.6 Elevation in combat

**7.6.1 Melee.** For each unit in the fight, add up the corners of the shared space that lie **behind it**, on its back side. The side with more gets **+1 Attack**, however large the difference. Holding the slope counts; how steep it is does not. A unit facing a corner has fewer corners behind it than one facing a side, and the rule takes no exception for that.<sup>[A16](#a16)</sup>

**7.6.2 Shooting.** Add up all four corners of the shooter's space and all four of the target's. A shooter standing higher gets **+1 Attack**; one standing lower takes **−1 Attack**.

> _A unit driven back uphill from a fight gains the slope if it is caught again. One pushed off a crest loses it. A ridge in mid-field swings the +1 to whoever holds it._

## 8. Commitments

A commitment is how you lean on a unit in a moment that matters. Throw a card out of your hand and that unit does a little better than it should have: one point of attack, of defence, of speed, of flexibility, of range — whichever the card names.

It is always optional, it is always worth exactly +1, and the card comes back at your next rally. A committed card is lent, not spent: until you rally, you cannot play it or commit it again.

**8.1** A **commitment** is discarding one card from your hand to squeeze +1 out of a unit for one action. Committed cards go to your **discard pile** and come back when you rally (§10.3).

**8.2** A card names the stat it may be committed for — normally exactly one, and no card names more than two. Committing gives **+1** to every stat the card names. The value is never anything but +1. Only stats the moment actually uses can matter, so a second stat on the card is simply ignored where it is no use.<sup>[B5](#b5)</sup>

**8.3** Commitment is **always optional** and always declinable, including when your opponent has already committed. Committing your last card empties your hand and loses the game (§11.1).

**8.4** Each moment accepts only certain stats, so a card is committable there only if it names at least one of them. A card whose only stat is Range is no use in a melee, and is not offered. Flexibility counts in a shot or a melee only for a unit at a deficit (§2.5.2); on ground it already handles, a Flexibility card adds nothing.<sup>[B6](#b6)</sup>

| Moment                                     | Stats it accepts                                                           | Who commits first             |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Before a movement order resolves (§4.3.10) | Speed, Flexibility                                                         | The moving player             |
| Defending a front engagement (§6.3.2)      | Speed, Flexibility                                                         | The defender                  |
| A ranged attack (§4.3.11)                  | Attack, Range or Flexibility (attacker); Defence or Flexibility (defender) | Attacker, then defender       |
| A melee (§7.2.2)                           | Attack, Defence, Flexibility                                               | First player, then the second |

**8.4.1** A commitment helps **your own unit only**. In a front engagement, the mover's Speed commitment (made before it moved) and the defender's are each added to their own unit before the two Speeds are compared (§6.3.2).

**8.5 Defence** as a commitment stat raises all three of a unit's thresholds by 1 for that combat, making it harder to make that unit retreat, reverse, or rout.<sup>[B3](#b3)</sup>

**8.6** A card in play (§4.1.6) cannot be committed. Only cards in hand can.

> _Example. Your hand holds **Brace** (commit stat: Defence) and **Strike** (commit stat: Attack). In a melee you commit Strike: +1 Attack for your unit. Next round you commit Brace instead. Both cards sit in your discard pile until you rally. **Stand and Fight** names two stats, Attack + Defence, and gives both (§8.2)._

**⚠ Edge case.** Commitments come back on your next rally, so a committed card is lent, not spent. What it costs you is the interval: until you rally, that card cannot be played, cannot be committed again, and cannot break a tie for initiative.

## 9. Retreat, reverse, rout

The three things an attack can do to a unit, in the order they are carried out (§7.5.2): retreat, then reverse, then rout. A unit that is going to rout can ignore the other two.

### 9.1 Retreat

**9.1.1** A retreat is a **backward move**, made with the unit's own Speed and Flexibility.

**9.1.2** A retreating unit must stay within **the region behind its starting position** (§2.3.3) — both the spaces it passes through and the space it ends in. It may not end where it started.

**9.1.3** A retreat must be the **smallest legal backward move available**: the fewest facing changes first, then the fewest steps. You do not get to use a retreat as a free repositioning. Where several moves tie for smallest, the owner chooses among them by where they end: the space and the facing. Two equally small retreats to one space, ending in different facings, are two choices; two equally small ways to the same space and facing are one retreat, not a choice. Fewest facing changes is minimum rotation, since every turn in a retreat is 45° (§9.1.4). Speed and Flexibility are the unit's current ones, with any Round Effect or Command modifiers (§7.1.5).

**9.1.4** A retreating unit **never turns about**: a unit forced back does not reverse to face the other way. Before its first step back it may turn by **at most 45°** in all. After the first step it turns 45° to either side per Flexibility spent, as often as its Flexibility lasts (§5.2.1).<sup>[A14](#a14)</sup>

**9.1.5** A unit **cannot retreat at all** if:

- an **unengaged enemy unit stands in one of its back spaces, facing it** — its line of withdrawal is cut; or
- no legal backward move exists — the board edge, friendly units it cannot pass, enemies or ground it cannot cover are in the way.

**9.1.6** A unit that is required to retreat and cannot, **routs** (§7.5, edge case).<sup>[B4](#b4)</sup>

**9.1.7** A retreating unit may not enter an enemy-occupied space, or a hidden stand (§3.6.4). A retreat never creates an engagement.

**9.1.8** A commander standing in the retreating unit's space **always** retreats with it, and ends in the unit's final space. There is no choice to stay behind. Only the unit's own commander goes: an enemy commander that rode into the space is with its own unit (§4.3.10), and stays.<sup>[A9](#a9)</sup>

**9.1.9** A retreat is an ordinary move for terrain (§5.5). A unit that cannot cover the ground behind it has no legal retreat, and routs (§9.1.6). A river at your back is a trap.<sup>[A3](#a3)</sup>

### 9.2 Reverse

A reverse stands for a unit that has been worn down, thrown into disorder or shaken, and is left in a vulnerable position until it is shored up. It is shown by turning the unit round because that works well in this system. It is not an about-face in meaning.

**9.2.1** The unit turns 180° where it stands. Nothing else changes.

**9.2.2** A reverse costs no Flexibility and is not a move.

**9.2.3** It cannot be applied to a unit still engaged (§7.5.5).

**9.2.4** Reverse is the mildest result on paper and often not in practice. A reversed unit's front becomes its back, so the enemy that just struck it is now standing behind it, and a rear engagement next round routs it outright (§6.3.3).

### 9.3 Rout

**9.3.1** A routed unit is **removed from the battle permanently**. It does not come back.

**9.3.2** Its owner immediately discards cards from hand equal to that unit's **Morale**, chosen freely from their hand. Morale 0 costs nothing.

**9.3.3** If several units of one player rout at the same moment — as happens at the support check (§10.4) — sum their Morale and discard that many at once. In a melee the two sides are separate: each owner pays for their own unit, the second player first, since that unit routs first (§7.5.6).

**9.3.4** If you cannot pay, you lose (§11.2).

**9.3.5 Commander casualty.** A commander standing in the space of a unit that routs in combat is defeated with it (§4.2.8). A unit that routs at the support check (§10.4) does not defeat it.<sup>[A15](#a15)</sup>

## 10. Cards: hand, piles, rally, support

There is one set of cards and it does everything: it is your hand, your losses, your recovery, and the measure of how much army you can keep on the table. Cards move between a few places, and only one of those moves is permanent.

Cards leave your hand four ways — played, committed, discarded to pay for a rout, or discarded in the bid before the battle. All of them come back when you rally. Rallying destroys one card, at random, forever. That burnt card, and any burnt during the bid (§3.3.1), are the only permanent losses in the whole system; and it is the reason a long battle grinds an army down.

### 10.1 The five places a card can be

| Place                                                    | In your hand? | Comes back?                   |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Hand**                                                 | yes           | —                             |
| **In play** — this round's card                          | no            | to the played pile at Cleanup |
| **Played pile** — cards played in earlier rounds         | no            | on rally, minus one burnt     |
| **Discard pile** — commitments, Morale payments and bids | no            | on rally, all of it           |
| **Burnt**                                                | no            | **never**                     |

**10.1.1** Only cards **in your hand** can be played or committed.

**10.1.2** Unit support, by contrast, does not depend on where a card was. The support check happens only in the instant after a rally (§10.4), and a rally has just gathered every card you still own into your hand. So a card provides its support unless it has been **burnt**. Burning is the only thing that takes support away.

### 10.2 How cards leave your hand

- **Played** in Phase 1 → in play → played pile.
- **Committed** for a bonus (§8) → discard pile.
- **Discarded to pay Morale** when a unit routs (§9.3) → discard pile.
- **Discarded in the bid** for colour (§3.3.1) → discard pile, or burnt if the bid runs past your hand.

A card is never burnt from your hand. The card burnt at a rally comes from your played pile (§10.3), and is gone for good.

### 10.3 Rallying

**10.3.1** In Cleanup, after this round's card has gone to the played pile, each player in turn, starting with the first player, may choose to rally. It is the **only** way to get cards back into your hand.

**10.3.2** To rally:

1. **Burn** one of your played cards, chosen at random. It is out of the game for good.
2. Take every other card in your played pile back into your hand.
3. Take your **entire discard pile** back into your hand.
4. Immediately perform the **unit support check** (§10.4).

**10.3.3** There is always something to burn: this round's card went to the played pile a moment ago (§4.5.1).

**10.3.4** If you decline to rally, nothing happens: your played pile keeps growing and **no support check occurs**. Units stay on the board regardless of whether your hand could hold them.

**10.3.5** Rallying costs you three things: one card destroyed for good, no say in which card that is, and a support check you may not be ready for. Against that, it is the only way to get your hand back, so the question is when, not whether.

**10.3.6** The first player finishes their rally entirely, support check and Morale payment included, before the second player decides whether to rally.

### 10.4 The unit support check

Your cards are what holds your army on the field. Each card can keep a certain number of units in good order — some cards will steady anything, most are particular about what they steady. When you rally, you check whether your cards can still account for everything you have on the board. Whatever they cannot account for breaks and leaves.

So it is a counting exercise, done once, in one go: _this is what my cards can hold, these are the units I have, anything left over is gone._

Three things make it less alarming than it sounds. It only happens when you rally, so you choose when to face it. Every card you own is in your hand at that moment, so nothing is missing from the count. And the number never drifts downward on its own — the only thing that shrinks it is burning, one card per rally.

#### The check in detail

**10.4.1 When.** Immediately after a rally, and only for the player who rallied (§10.3.2). At no other time.

**10.4.2 What a card holds.** Each card names one kind of unit it can steady, and how many:

- **any unit at all** — _generic ×1_, on every generic card
- a **trait** — _formation ×2_, on every trait card
- a **named unit type** — on unit cards, which are still being designed

A card always holds at least one place, so every card in your hand contributes.

**10.4.3 Add them up into pools.** Cards naming the same thing combine. Two cards each holding _formation ×2_ make a single pool of _formation ×4_. Cards naming different things stay separate: a _phalanx_ pool and a _generic_ pool are two pools, not one.

**10.4.4 Assign your units to the pools.** You do this yourself: for each pool, name which of your units fill its places. Every unit on your board needs a place in one pool. A unit may take **only one** place, and only in a pool it matches — Punic Citizen Spearmen can go in a _formation_ pool, a _phalanx_ pool, a _melee_ pool or a _generic_ pool, but into exactly one of them. A pool need not be filled if you have no unit that matches it.

**10.4.5 Use what you have.** If a place is still open and any uncovered unit could fill it, you must fill it. You are **not** required to find the arrangement that saves the most units. Where a unit could go into either a narrow pool or a broad one, that is your call, and a bad call is allowed to cost you.

**10.4.6 Anything left over routs.** Every unit with no place removes itself from the board, all at once (§9.3.3).

**10.4.7 Pay for them together.** Add up the Morale of everything that just routed and discard that many cards. If you cannot, you lose (§11.2).

> _Your cards hold: **Form Up** (formation ×2), **Dash** (generic ×1), **Strike** (generic ×1) — four places in two pools._
> _Your board has five units: 3 Manipular Legions (formation), 1 Velites, 1 Equites._
>
> _Five units, four places. Something is going to break; the only question is what._
>
> _The formation pool takes two Legions. The generic pool's two places can take any two of the third Legion, the Velites and the Equites. Put the Legion and the Equites there, and the Velites rout. Pay their Morale, which is nothing._
>
> _Put the Velites in the generic pool instead, and a Legion or the Equites routs — two Morale instead of none. That assignment is perfectly legal; §10.4.5 does not oblige you to find the better one._

**10.4.8 What this costs you over a battle.** Nothing you commit and nothing you pay in Morale reduces your support, because those cards return. Only burning does, and you burn exactly one card per rally. An army's capacity to hold itself together therefore declines slowly, predictably, and entirely as a consequence of how often you have needed to rally.

## 11. Ending the game

Your hand is your army's will to fight. The game ends the instant it gives out. There is no grace period.

**11.1 An empty hand loses at once.** The moment your hand is empty, you **lose**. It does not matter how many cards wait in your played pile or discard: an army with nothing in hand has nothing left to fight with. That includes playing your last card in Phase 1 and committing your last card (§8).<sup>[B7](#b7)</sup>

**11.2 A cost you cannot survive.** If you must discard cards for Morale (§9.3) and the number owed is **equal to or greater than** the cards in your hand, you **lose immediately**. Paying with your last card is already a loss, which is §11.1 applied: you must survive the payment, not merely make it. A Morale debt of zero costs nothing.

**11.3 Both at once.** If both players' hands become empty at the same moment, which can only happen when both play their last card in Phase 1, the player who **held initiative going into the round** wins, since the cards were committed before they were revealed.<sup>[B7](#b7)</sup>

**11.4 No units left.** A player with no units on the board **loses immediately**, however many cards they hold. This will almost never come up, and it is not there for the cases where it does: it closes the door on feeding your own army to the enemy so that you can go on cycling a small hand indefinitely.<sup>[B8](#b8)</sup>

**11.5** There is no other way to win: no points, no objectives, no territory. Wiping out an army does end the game (§11.4), but as an accident rather than a plan. Long before that, the fighting has usually cost your opponent the cards they needed.

**11.6 An empty hand is never safe.** Before you play, commit or pay, count what will be left in your hand. If the answer is none, you lose. Rallying at Cleanup (§10.3) is how you get your cards back, so rally while you still have some.

### 12.3 Quick tables

**Initiative** — card values run 1 to 4. Lower wins; tie goes to the current holder.

**Movement costs**

|                                   | Cost                     |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| One step, orthogonal or diagonal  | 1 Speed                  |
| One turn: 45° either way, or 180° | 1 Flexibility            |
| Passing through a friendly unit   | combined Flexibility ≥ 6 |

**Ground** — deficit = terrain threshold − Flexibility. A deficit is a penalty to Attack, thresholds, Speed and Range. A move takes the worst threshold and the worst speed limit it touches: cap Speed at the limit, subtract the deficit, then add modifiers (§5.5).

| Road | Open | Fields, ford | Scrub, stony, mud | Light forest, crags, bank, marsh | Dense forest |
| ---- | ---- | ------------ | ----------------- | -------------------------------- | ------------ |
| 0    | 1    | 2            | 3                 | 4                                | 5            |

Speed limits: fields, scrub, stony 3 · ford, mud, light forest, crags, bank 2 · marsh, dense forest 1.

**Elevation** — melee: more corners behind you, +1 Attack. Shooting: higher space +1 Attack, lower −1.

**Line of sight** — any corner to any corner. Blocked by forest and by ground higher than both ends; touching counts. Units never block. Adjacent always sees.

**Engagement type** — engaging unit's facing vs. defender's facing

| Same or 45° off                   | 90° off                                 | Opposite                                     |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Rear** — defender routs at once | **Flank** — defender wheels, no retreat | **Front** — defender may fall back if faster |

**Attack value** — Attack + Round Effect + Command modifiers + committed card − deficit ± elevation + support.

**Melee support**

| Fighting unit is in supporter's… |        |
| -------------------------------- | ------ |
| front spaces                     | **+2** |
| flanking spaces                  | **+1** |
| back spaces                      | —      |

Supporter must be adjacent, unengaged, not behind the fighter, and not across a blocked diagonal (both side spaces held by enemies).

**Ranged support** — +1 per supporter, flat. A supporter must have been ordered by the same command, must be able to make the shot itself, and spends its own shot to support. The target must be unengaged.

**Combat results** — retreat, then reverse, then rout; in a melee, both retreats, then both reverses, then both routs

| Retreat                | Reverse                             | Rout                |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| smallest backward move | turn 180° (not while still engaged) | removed; pay Morale |

A unit that will rout can ignore its own retreat and reverse. A forced retreat with nowhere to go becomes a rout. In each stage of a melee the first player's blow lands first, so the second player's unit suffers first.

**Commitments** — +1 to each stat the card names, always optional. Each moment accepts only some stats:

| Before moving      | Front-engagement defence | Shooting / being shot                             | Melee                        |
| ------------------ | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Speed, Flexibility | Speed, Flexibility       | Attack, Range, Flexibility / Defence, Flexibility | Attack, Defence, Flexibility |

**You lose when** your hand is empty at any moment, whether you played, committed or paid away your last card, or when you have no units left on the board. If both hands empty at once, initiative wins.

## 13. Index of awkward situations

Things that come up, in the order players tend to hit them.

**"Can I turn and then charge in the same order?"** Yes. Turn (1 Flexibility), step, and turn again after the step if you have Flexibility left. Turns and steps can come in any order. §5.2.2

**"Can I turn twice on the spot?"** Yes, if you have the Flexibility. Each turn costs 1, so two 45° turns wheel the unit 90°. §5.2.2

**"My unit has Flexibility 0."** It cannot turn, ever, under any circumstances short of a card giving it +1. It also cannot be ordered to make a front engagement unless it already happens to be squared up to the target. §5.2.3, §6.2.3

**"Can I walk around their flank and hit them in the back?"** Not in one order. A rear engagement requires that you were _already_ behind them when the move began. §6.2.2

**"They have a unit in my way and I'm faster."** You can pass through friendlies if the combined Flexibility is 6 or more. You can never pass through an enemy, and you can never pass through a space where two units are engaged. §5.4

**"Both units routed each other in melee."** Yes, that happens. Melee is simultaneous in strength; the routs are carried out second player's unit first, since the first player's blow lands first, and each owner pays Morale. §7.2.1, §7.5.6

**"My unit has to retreat but it's against the board edge."** It routs. §7.5, §9.1.6

**"There's an enemy behind my unit — can it still retreat?"** Yes, unless that enemy is right at your back, unengaged, and facing your unit. Then the retreat is cut off entirely. §9.1.5

**"Can I retreat further than necessary to get somewhere useful?"** No. A retreat is always the smallest legal backward move: fewest turns, then fewest steps. §9.1.3

**"My unit was hit hard enough to reverse, but it didn't turn."** It was still sharing a space with an enemy when its turn came. Only a unit that has fallen back, or whose enemy has gone, turns about. §7.5.5

**"Can my archers shoot through my own line?"** Yes. Units never block line of sight; only ground does. §7.4.6

**"Can I shoot into the wood at their skirmishers?"** Not unless you are adjacent. The target's own space counts as blocking, so a unit in a forest can see out but cannot be seen in. §7.4.6

**"My phalanx won't go into the fields."** A unit with Speed 1 and Flexibility 1 stops dead at a terrain threshold of 2. Give it +1 Speed or +1 Flexibility, by card or commitment, or go round. §5.5.2

**"Who has initiative in the first round?"** White, always. In a full setup Black has chosen the battlefield in exchange, and deploys first. On a bare board White deploys first, and initiative is the compensation. §3.3.2, §3.5.4

**"Can my archers shoot at a unit that is already engaged?"** No. The target must be alone in its space. §7.4.2

**"Can my archers shoot while engaged?"** No. §4.3.11

**"Do my archers shoot automatically?"** No. Shooting only happens when a Shoot command orders it. A round in which you play a Move card is a round in which nobody shoots. §4.3.11

**"Can I command the same unit twice?"** No — once per round, whether alone or as part of a line. §4.3.6

**"Can a line include a unit I already commanded?"** No, and the line stops there rather than jumping over it. §4.3.6

**"How far does a line reach from my general?"** The end you name first must be within inspiration range. The rest of the line need only match the trait and type restrictions — it can run right off into the distance. §4.3.5

**"My card says 4 units. Do I have to order four?"** No. It is a maximum. You may order fewer, or none. §4.3.2

**"Do the command's modifiers last into the melee phase?"** Yes, for the whole round, for the units that were actually commanded. §4.3.8

**"Does the round effect apply to units I didn't command?"** Yes — that is exactly what distinguishes it from the command's modifiers. It applies to any of your units that satisfy _its_ restrictions. §4.3.7

**"Can I commit the card I played this round?"** No. Only cards in hand. §8.6

**"Do committed cards come back?"** Yes, on your next rally, along with everything else in your discard pile. Only the one card burnt during a rally is gone forever. §10.3

**"Can I play or commit my last card?"** You can, and you lose on the spot. An empty hand is an instant loss. Keep a card back. §11.1, §11.6

**"Can I rally with an empty hand?"** No. An empty hand has already lost the game. You can rally with a single card left: your played pile always holds at least this round's card to burn. §10.3, §11.1<sup>[B7](#b7)</sup>

**"If I never rally, do I ever lose units to the support check?"** No. The support check happens only after a rally. Declining to rally is a way to keep units on the board with a hand too thin to justify them — for exactly as long as you can afford to hold cards. §10.3.4

**"Does my opponent's rally affect my units?"** No. Each player checks only their own. §10.4

**"Two of my units both qualify for the last support place."** Your choice, and you are not required to make the choice that saves the most units. §10.4.5

**"I have exactly as many cards as the Morale I owe."** You lose. You must survive the payment, not merely make it. §11.2

**"Does a Defence commitment protect my unit in a melee?"** Yes: +1 to all three of its thresholds for that fight. It does nothing to the enemy's blow. §8.5<sup>[B3](#b3)</sup>

## 14. Glossary

**Adjacent** — orthogonally or diagonally touching; up to eight spaces. §2.4.1

**Arc** — the widening cone in front of a unit, used for shooting. §2.4.3

**Bid** — the cards each player discards before the battle for the choice of colour. §3.3.1

**Attack value** — the total a unit brings to one combat: Attack plus modifiers plus support. §7.1

**Back spaces** — the three spaces behind a unit. §2.3.2

**Burn** — to remove a card from the game permanently. Happens once per rally. §10.3

**Commander** — the general piece. No facing, no combat value; the origin of inspiration range. §4.2

**Commit** — to discard a card from hand for +1 to the stat it names, for one action. Always optional. §8

**Deficit** — how far a unit's Flexibility falls short of its ground's terrain threshold. A penalty to every other stat while it stands there. §2.5.2

**Discard pile** — cards spent on commitments, Morale and bids. Returns entirely on a rally. §10.1

**Elevation** — high, level or low, marked on each corner of a space. §2.6

**Engagement** — two enemy units locked in the same space. Created in Phase 3, fought in Phase 4. §6

**Flanking spaces** — the two spaces directly left and right of a unit. §2.3.2

**Flexibility** — how many times a unit may change facing in one move. Also governs passing through friends. §5.1

**Front spaces** — the three spaces a unit faces. §2.3.2

**In play** — the card you revealed this round. Not in hand and cannot be committed. §4.1.6

**Initiative** — acting first. Won by the lower card value; kept on a tie. §4.1

**Inspiration range** — how far from your commander a command or round effect reaches. §4.3.4

**Line** — a contiguous rank of 1–8 friendly units standing shoulder to shoulder, all facing the same direction or its opposite. §4.3.5

**Morale** — how many cards a unit's rout costs its owner. Also the army-building floor. §3.1.6, §9.3

**Played pile** — cards played in previous rounds, awaiting a rally. §10.1

**Pool** — all the support places your cards grant for one kind of unit, added together. §10.4.3

**Rally** — burn one card from the played pile, take everything else back, then check support. §10.3

**Reverse** — turned 180° in place. §9.2

**Rout** — removed from the battle; pay Morale. §9.3

**Scenario card** — a deployment map with primary and secondary terrain. Part of your army list, never part of your hand. §3.2

**Round Effect** — a standing modifier from your in-play card, applying to qualifying units whether commanded or not. §4.3.7

**Speed limit** — a cap on a unit's printed Speed for any move that touches that terrain, applied before deficit and modifiers. §2.5.1, §5.5.2

**Stand** — a numbered marker for a hidden unit, or a decoy. §3.6

**Support (combat)** — the bonus adjacent friends lend to a fight. §7.3

**Support (unit)** — the places your cards offer to keep units on the board, filled by you after a rally. A unit with no place routs. §10.4

**Terrain threshold** — how badly a kind of ground disorders a formation. Not to be confused with a unit's combat thresholds. §2.5.1

**Threshold** — Retreat, Reverse, or Rout. Met when the attack value equals or exceeds it. Results are carried out retreat, then reverse, then rout. §7.5

**Traits** — the groups cards use to name units: _formation, phalanx, warband, skirmish, cavalry, chariot, beast, shock, melee, missile_ and _javelin_. A unit usually has several. §10.4.2

# APPENDIX — WHERE THE DIGITAL EDITION DIFFERS

Each entry is keyed to a mark in the rules. It says what the rule is, what the rules engine does instead, and what would close the gap. The entries come in four kinds:

- **Code lags rule:** the rule is settled and the engine has not caught up.
- **Bug:** the engine contradicts its own rule, or crashes.
- **Code and rule disagree:** both are deliberate and they conflict. One must give.
- **Rule open:** the design itself is not settled, and the engine has picked a reading for now.

"Probe" means the behaviour was run against the engine on 2026-11-08; otherwise it was read. Paths are under `src/domain/`.

## A — Board, setup, orders, movement, engagement

<a id="a1"></a>**A1. Engaged units are offered for orders, then skipped.** _Closed: S19._

- **Rule:** §4.3.4: an engaged unit cannot be ordered. §4.3.5: an engaged unit **breaks the line** (author, 2026-11-08, superseding a same-day ruling that the line runs past it and counts it toward its eight — commanding a line must mean every unit in it is commanded, and the looser rule returns only if play-tests ask for it). Draft 3 §16.13 left it open.
- **Code does:** Issue-time eligibility never checks engagement, so an engaged unit is offered alone, as a line end and as a line member (`legality/choiceOptions/issueCommand/getLegalUnitsForIssueCommand.ts`; `getLegalLineEndsForIssueCommand.ts`, `canJoin`). Movement skips it at resolve (`getLegalMoveUnits.ts`; filter added in `778f05c`), so a "units" order can be spent on it for nothing. Probe: an engaged unit at E-5 was offered at issue and refused at resolve. A unit engaged inside a rank joins the line and is skipped at resolve; under the rule as now settled, all three offerings are wrong — the line should stop at it.
- **To settle:** Treat an engaged unit as non-qualifying at issue time everywhere: single-unit orders, line ends, and line membership (`canJoin`), so a line stops where an engagement stands.
- **Closed:** `getLegalUnitsForIssueCommand` no longer offers a unit in an engaged space, alone or as a line's start, and `canJoinLine` refuses one, so a line stops where an engagement stands. `canJoinLine` also refuses a unit already commanded this round (§4.3.6).

<a id="a2"></a>**A2. One Flexibility reaches any facing; the rule is 45° or 180°.** _Closed 2026-11-08._

- **Rule:** §5.2.1: a turn is 45° either way, or 180° about. (Author decision 2026-11-08; `AUDIT.md`, Movement item 1.)
- **Closed:** `getFacingsOneTurnAway` states the rule, and `exploreUnitMoves` now walks a uniform graph — a step edge and a turn edge from every state, repeatable in any order (§5.2.2) within the two allowances (slice S3, `82f4a77`). A 90° wheel costs two spends, so the Flexibility-1 phalanx stays stiff, and the free sidestep is gone. Retreat turning (A14) closed with it.

<a id="a3"></a>**A3. Terrain does nothing yet.** _Code lags rule._ (`ROADMAP.md` §1; `AUDIT.md`, Movement item 3.)

- **Rule:** §2.5, §5.5, §9.1.9: twelve terrains, each with a terrain threshold and a speed limit; a deficit penalises every stat but Flexibility; a move takes the worst threshold and limit it touches, caps Speed, subtracts the deficit, then adds modifiers; a diagonal pays one corner space's terrain, mover's choice. Closed design (`ROADMAP.md` §1).
- **Code does:** The terrain list is `plain, rocks, scrub, lightForest, denseForest`, with no numbers attached (`entities/board/terrainTypes.ts`). The terrain step in `getCurrentUnitStat` adds nothing (`queries/unit/getCurrentUnitStat.ts`). `canMoveThrough` answers only yes or no, so a move has no cost to compare (`legality/unitMovement/passage/canMoveThrough.ts`, `checkDiagonalMove.ts`, `exploreUnitMoves.ts`). Retreats already go through `exploreUnitMoves`, so terrain should reach them for free (`getLegalRetreats.ts`).
- **To settle:** Follow `ROADMAP.md` §6.1 in order: terrain types with their two numbers, then the deficit in `getCurrentUnitStat` (Range included, Flexibility excluded), then movement cost. Add retreat specs for a river at the back. The deficit applies everywhere a stat is read, the front-engagement Speed comparison included (§6.3.2; author, 2026-11-08). The string terrain types and the eight-direction water cover (`entities/board/waterCover.ts`) are placeholders and can go: water is a low corner on a bank or ford.

<a id="a4"></a>**A4. Commanders can share a space with the enemy commander.** _Settled: allowed._

- **Rule:** More than one thing may stand in a space if one is a commander. A commander may not enter a space holding an enemy **unit**. Two commanders in one space is legal (author, 2026-11-08: "can't really argue against it").
- **Code does:** Commander movement only refuses enemy units, so it may end on, or pass through, a space holding the enemy commander (`legality/commanderMovement/exploreCommanderMoves.ts`, `exploreCommanderMoves`, `hasEnemyUnit`). That is now the rule. Confirmed with the A15 work: two commanders sharing a space where one side's unit routs, and only that side's commander falls (`defeatCommanderWithRoutedUnit.test.ts`).
- **Still open:** what happens when an enemy **unit** enters a commander's space — see Open design questions.

<a id="a5"></a>**A5. The setup sequence does not exist; the engine gives Black initiative and has White deploy first.** _Code lags rule._

- **Rule:** §3.2–3.5: scenario cards bought with the army, a bid in discarded cards for colour, White's veto, Black's scenario, White's secondary terrain, alternating placement, then Black deploys and White deploys last. White holds initiative in round one. Closed in outline (`ROADMAP.md` §4). Three scenario cards per list, public before the bid (author, 2026-11-08). The card format is deferred.
- **Code does:** Setup is one step: White places all units, then Black, in a fixed three-row belt marked "KISS provisional" (`expected/expectedEvent/byPhase/getExpectedSetupUnitsEvent.ts`; `legality/choiceOptions/setupUnits/getSetupZoneCoordinates.ts`). Black starts with initiative (`factories/game/createEmptyGameState.ts`: "Black always begins the game with the initiative"). No bid, scenario, veto or placement phase exists, and the army schema has no scenario cards.
- **To settle:** Swap the deployment order and the starting initiative now; they are one-line changes and the rest of the sequence depends on them. Then build the phases in `ROADMAP.md` §4.2. Run the paper test of setup time (`ROADMAP.md` §6.3) before the engine work. On a bare board the engine's order is already right (White first); only the starting initiative changes. In a full setup Black deploys first.

<a id="a6"></a>**A6. Inspiration range: the stand-in cards print a range almost everywhere.** _Closed by decision 2026-11-09 (S20)._ (Draft §16.3.)

- **Rule:** §4.2.7: a range printed **—** is unrestricted; 0 is the commander's own space; "all" on a round effect reaches every unit of its trait. Most generic commands and many trait commands print —. (Cycle 1 roster, "Generic cards" notes.)
- **Code does:** A negative value (−1, the schema minimum) means unrestricted, which matches — (`entities/card/restrictions.ts`; `getLegalUnitsForIssueCommand.ts`, `unitMatchesInspirationRange`; `queries/unit/getCurrentUnitStat.ts`). The commands are checked once, when issued; round effects are checked every time their stat is read, from wherever the unit then stands — for a move's Speed and Flexibility, where the move began (§5.1.4), so the engine's start-space read during the engagement contest is right by design (see A11).
- **Closed:** Nothing to build. "All of a trait" is a trait restriction with range −1: every unit of that trait, wherever it stands. The −1 is intentional (author, 2026-11-09): the authoring interface keeps the range a numeric field, and −1, 0 and the positive ranges are one family with distinct meanings. The stand-in deck printing 0–4 everywhere is a fixture, not drift. The range test written twice (`TODO(composition)` in `getCurrentUnitStat.ts`) moves to Pass B.

<a id="a7"></a>**A7. Which eight units a long rank offers depends on where you start; the line rule exists twice.** _Closed: S18._

- **Rule:** A line is 1 to 8 units, named by its two ends.
- **Code does:** The engine builds the contiguous rank around the start unit, stretching first to the start's left (up to seven units) and then to its right until eight are gathered, so in a rank longer than eight some end units cannot be named from some starts (`getLegalLineEndsForIssueCommand.ts`, `getLineSegmentFromStart`; checked again in `validation/playerChoice/isValidIssueCommandEvent.ts`). A second line rule, which lists **every** eight-unit window that contains a unit, is exported but called by no one (`queries/line/getLinesFromUnit.ts`; `TODO(composition)`). `isValidLine` (`legality/choiceOptions/issueCommand/isValidLine.ts`) is a third statement of the shape.
- **To settle:** §4.3.5 says you choose which eight, which is the window rule. Keep `getLinesFromUnit`'s windows (or equivalent), retire the greedy cap, and keep one statement of the line shape.
- **Closed:** One rule: `canJoinLine` says who may stand in a line and `getLineSegmentContainingUnit` gives the unbroken rank; `getLegalLineEndsForIssueCommand` offers every unit within seven places of the start, so any eight can be chosen. Validation checks membership against those ends and the rank between them. The greedy cap, `getLinesFromUnit` and its helpers, and `isValidLine` are gone.

<a id="a8"></a>**A8. The mover never gets to commit.** _Closed 2026-11-09._

- **Rule:** §4.3.10: before a unit moves, its owner may commit for +1 Speed or Flexibility; that Speed also counts in a front-engagement contest (§8.4.1). Draft 3 says so, and the engine has a built branch for it.
- **Closed:** A Move order is now carried out one choice per decision (author, 2026-11-09): `chooseMovingUnit` names the unit and opens its move, `commitToMovement` takes the mover's card or its refusal, and `moveUnit` writes the path (slice S9). The committed card counts toward the move's budget (`createMoveContext`, through `getCurrentUnitStat`'s modifiers), so validation accepts a path only the card makes possible, and the front-engagement Speed contest reads the same commitment. A scenario spec plays the order through the engines.

<a id="a9"></a>**A9. A commander may ride with a unit; the engine checks a flag for it and never moves the commander.** _Closed: S10 (moves, retreats); the fall with a routed unit is S21._

- **Rule:** §4.3.10: a commander in a unit's space may move with it when the unit moves, and ends in the unit's final space. §9.1.8: when the unit retreats, its commander always goes with it (author, 2026-11-09: no choice).
- **Code does:** The `moveUnit` choice has a `moveCommander` flag. Validation accepts it only when the player's commander shares the unit's space (`validation/playerChoice/isValidMoveUnitEvent.ts`), and `carryRidingCommander` (`transforms/pureTransforms/commanders/`) carries the commander to the unit's final space: on open ground in `applyMoveUnitEvent`, into a rear engagement in `applyStartEngagementEvent`, and into a front or flank engagement in `applyCompleteUnitMovementEvent`. Retreats carry the commander too, with no flag: `applyResolveRetreatEvent` moves the retreating unit's own commander with it. The commander's fall with a routed unit is S21.
- **To settle:** Apply the flag by moving the commander to the unit's destination, and give retreats the same option. The engagement case is decided (author, 2026-11-08): the ride into an engagement is allowed, as the one exception to §2.2.3/§4.2.4 — the commander is committed to the fight and falls with its unit if that unit routs (§4.2.8, friendly rout only; the enemy's rout does not touch it).

<a id="a10"></a>**A10. Units and the "blocked diagonal" test do not agree with commanders and support.** _Settled: two rules, by design._

- **Rule:** §2.4.4: two enemies on the flanking orthogonals block a diagonal.
- **Code does:** Commanders and melee support use exactly that (`queries/board/diagonalIsClear.ts`; called from `exploreCommanderMoves.ts` and `getMeleeSupportValue.ts`). Units use a stricter test: at least one of the two orthogonals must be passable by that unit, so an enemy, an engaged space, or a friend too stiff to pass through counts against it (`legality/unitMovement/passage/checkDiagonalMove.ts`). Two stiff friends can therefore block a diagonal for a unit, and cannot for a commander or for support. The destination must also be passable by the same test, which forbids ending a diagonal step on an enemy (see A12). A comment on `diagonalIsClear` is marked `TODO(misleading)`.
- **To settle:** Decided (author, 2026-11-08): both rules are intended. Movement is blocked by what the mover cannot pass through — certain friendlies, and later terrain; support and commanders ask only the enemy question. §2.4.4 now says so, and the engine already plays both. What remains is the `TODO(misleading)` comment on `diagonalIsClear` and, someday, distinct names for the two tests — nomenclature, not design.

<a id="a11"></a>**A11. The mover is not in the shared space until the engagement is set up; its Speed is measured from where it began.** _Closed 2026-11-09._ (`AUDIT.md`, "Movement (not settled)" item 2.)

- **Rule:** Decided (author, 2026-11-08; refined the same day). The move event carries the unit's **full trajectory, atomically**, encoded as **deltas** — step, turn 45° either way, turn about. The spaces resolve through one owned fold; a delta list cannot encode an illegal shape, and a raw "manual" client input (turn/reverse/step) produces the identical event an assisted UI does. The mover genuinely advances along its path: before an engagement is set up it stands in the last space of its path before the defender's, which ends the half-applied move. Its **movement stats do not travel with it**: Speed and Flexibility are sampled once, where its move began (§5.1.4) — this supersedes the same-day ruling that the Speed contest reads the penultimate space. One move is one act: no mid-move stat discontinuity, no benefit for clipping an aura en route, and the contest stays symmetric, since the defender samples at the start of its own prospective retreat (§6.3.2). Legal moves reach the client as compound atoms, never an enumeration of whole trajectories; validation folds the submitted deltas through the same step and turn predicates. Terrain, when it lands, binds to the trodden path — worst touched (A3) — and because the worst can only worsen, a partial path once illegal stays illegal, so per-delta validation stays sound.
- **Code does (S8, 2026-11-09):** The `moveUnit` event carries `path` — step, turnLeft, turnRight, turnAbout — in place of a destination (author, 2026-11-09: the destination is dropped, and the squaring turn of an oblique front approach is written into the path). `traceMovePath` validates it through the same step, turn and ending rules the move search uses; every legal move the search offers comes with its path (`getLegalUnitMoves`), and the transform reads the destination by geometry alone (`getPlacementAfterPath`). The retreat choice does not carry a path yet (Cluster 3).
- **Settled 2026-11-09 (author):** the mover does **not** stand in the penultimate space before the engagement is set up — given §5.1.4, that would be misleading. It waits on its start space, where its movement stats were read, until the engagement places it; the path on the event carries the motion for anyone animating it. This supersedes the 2026-11-08 ruling that the mover "genuinely advances" before the contest.

<a id="a12"></a>**A12. A diagonal step can never end on an enemy.** _Closed 2026-11-08._

- **Rule:** §6.2.1: engaging from a flanking space is always allowed, straight or diagonal, and a diagonal step passes the usual corner test (§5.4.5). Nothing about the engagement is special for being diagonal.
- **Closed:** `checkDiagonalMove` now asks only the corner question, as its own header always said it should (slice S4); what the unit may do at the target is the walk's question, and the walk judges an enemy there as an engagement. A diagonal-facing defender can be taken in the flank, spec-pinned, and two enemies on the corners still shut the step.

<a id="a13"></a>**A13. The squaring-up turn is checked, never made; a 135° approach crashes.** _Closed 2026-11-08._

- **Rule:** §6.2.3: a front approach ends facing directly opposite, spending 1 Flexibility if needed. §6.3.1 then always classifies.
- **Closed:** `getEngagementEndFacing` states the ending — squared head-on for a front approach, the point spent when the step arrived oblique; flank and rear fight as they arrived — and the walk applies it (slice S5), so the oblique ending is never offered and §6.3.1 always classifies. The throw in `generateStartEngagementEvent` stays as the guard on that guarantee.

<a id="a14"></a>**A14. A retreat never turns about.** _Closed 2026-11-09._

- **Rule:** §9.1.4: a retreat may turn at most 45° at its start; after the first step it turns 45° either way per spend, repeatable, and never about (author, 2026-11-09: "reversing as part of a retreat is explicitly not an option"). This replaces the 2026-11-08 ruling that after the first step turning followed §5.2.1, about included (with A2: the limited turn is back, so sequential turns are back in).
- **Closed:** The retreat walks the same uniform graph as the advance (slice S6, with S3, `82f4a77`): its opening turn is one 45° neighbour at most, and no retreat turn is ever about (`getTurnOptions`); specs pin the wheel priced at 45° a spend and the missing about-face after a step.

<a id="a15"></a>**A15. Commanders are never defeated.** _Closed: S21, 2026-11-09._

- **Rule:** §4.2.8: a commander is defeated if it stands in a space where one of its own units routs in combat, and not when a unit routs at the support check. Defeat costs nothing on its face — no discard, no Morale; the commander leaves the board, and every inspiration range in that hand reaches nothing (author, 2026-11-08).
- **Code did:** The game state carried `lostCommanders`, and `addCommanderToLostCommanders` existed, but nothing in production called it: a rout never touched a commander.
- **Closed:** Every combat rout — a shot's or a melee's result, a retreat with nowhere to go, a rear engagement — leaves the board through `removeUnitsLeavingUnderRout`, which now fells the routing unit's own commander if it stands there (`defeatCommanderWithRoutedUnit`); an enemy commander in the same space stays. The support check's rout does not pass through it. A missing commander was already survivable where ranges are read: a range of 0 or more reaches nothing, and an unrestricted (−1) card still applies. Move Commanders passes over a side whose commander has fallen (`getMoveCommandersStepFrom`, §4.2.1). Specs: `defeatCommanderWithRoutedUnit.test.ts`, `getMoveCommandersStepFrom.test.ts`, and `scenarios/rearEngagement.test.ts`, where the defender's commander falls with it.

<a id="a16"></a>**A16. Elevation is a 0–5 height per corner and nothing reads it.** _Code lags rule._

- **Rule:** §2.6, §7.6: each corner is +1, 0 or −1, with no alternating corners. In melee, the side with more corners behind it gets +1 Attack; in shooting, the higher space gets +1 Attack and the lower −1. Closed design (`ROADMAP.md` §1.3). The melee bonus is to Attack (author, 2026-11-08).
- **Code does:** Corners are integers 0 to 5 (`entities/board/elevation.ts`) and no rule reads them.
- **To settle:** Change the corner range to −1, 0, +1, enforce no alternating corners, then add both combat comparisons.

<a id="a17"></a>**A17. Hidden units do not exist.** _Code lags rule._

- **Rule:** §3.6: numbered stands and decoys placed in hidden deployment spaces; they cannot move and block all movement; revealed by sight, adjacency, the owner's choice after Phase 2, or a unit moving in. Closed design (`ROADMAP.md` §3). The number of stands per colour is deferred.
- **Code does:** Nothing. The visibility-split state (`GameStateVisibility`) that hides hands is the obvious home for a stand's identity.
- **To settle:** Build after line of sight, which decides what is hidden.

<a id="a18"></a>**A18. No line-of-sight check.** _Code lags rule._

- **Rule:** §7.4.6: trace any corner to any corner; forest and higher ground block on any contact; the target's own space counts; adjacent always sees; units never block. Closed design (`ROADMAP.md` §2).
- **Code does:** Targeting checks side, the attacker's engagement, Range and the arc only (`legality/choiceOptions/performRangedAttack/canUnitRangedAttackTarget.ts`, "No line-of-sight check"). The engaged-target refusal lives elsewhere (`getLegalRangedAttackTargets`, `hasSingleUnit`).
- **To settle:** Add corner-to-corner tracing after elevation lands, since higher ground blocks. Move the engaged-target refusal into the same predicate, as `ROADMAP.md` §6.1 suggests.

## B — Combat, commitments, cards, endgame

<a id="b1"></a>**B1. The target of a shot is never offered a Defence card.** _Closed: S15._

- **Rule:** §4.3.11, §7.4.5: the defender may commit for Defence. (Author decision 2026-11-08.)
- **Code does:** `generateResolveRangedAttackEvent` would apply it, but `getLegalCommitToRangedAttackEvents` offers both sides the same list (Range, Attack, Flexibility), so only a two-stat card that happens to name Defence could ever protect a target.
- **Closed:** The shooter and the target now commit through separate events with separate lists: `commitToRangedAttack` (Range, Attack, Flexibility), the shooter's one window before it aims; `commitToRangedDefense` (Defence, Flexibility), the target's, once it is named. A target's Flexibility counts because a deficit penalises its thresholds too (§2.5.2).

<a id="b2"></a>**B2. Attack value is never clamped at zero.** _Closed: S16._

- **Rule:** §7.1.4: **every stat floors at zero**, the attack value included (author, 2026-11-08). The floor is applied once, to the finished total — never mid-sum, so a floored stat cannot be rebounded above its raw arithmetic by a later bonus.
- **Code does:** sums base, modifiers and support with no floor. `getCurrentUnitStat` returns `baseStat + totalModifier` and `applyAttackValue` compares the raw sum. A negative value fails every threshold, including a printed Reverse of 0, whereas a value of exactly 0 meets it. (`src/domain/queries/unit/getCurrentUnitStat.ts`, `src/domain/queries/attack/applyAttackValue.ts:14`)
- **To settle:** Floor the finished total in `getCurrentUnitStat` and `applyAttackValue`, so a negative attack behaves as 0 and still reverses a 0-threshold unit. One wrinkle: the engine encodes an **unrestricted** inspiration range as −1 (A6), while the author reads a negative range as _no range_. The sentinel must be exempted from the floor or re-encoded before range is ever modified — today nothing modifies it, so the collision is latent.
- **Closed:** `getCurrentUnitStat` floors every finished stat at zero. The attack value is a unit's Attack plus support, so the procedures sum `getRawUnitStat` (the unfloored stat) and `applyAttackValue` floors the whole once: a blow summing to −2 still meets a Reverse of 0, and meets nothing above it. The inspiration-range sentinel is a command-card value, not a unit stat (author, 2026-11-09), so the floor never reaches it.

<a id="b3"></a>**B3. Defence commitments do nothing in melee.** _Closed: S14._

- **Rule:** §8.5: a Defence commitment raises its own unit's thresholds by 1 for that combat, in melee as in ranged.
- **Code does:** `generateResolveMeleeEvent` passes no defending modifiers to `applyAttackValue`, so a melee Defence card is discarded for nothing (`procedures/resolveAttack/generateResolveMeleeEvent.ts`; `TODO(wrong)`; unchanged since `f69a8d1`). The ranged path does pass them, and the old rulebook lists Defence as a melee commit.
- **Closed:** `generateResolveMeleeEvent` passes each side's own commitment as that unit's defending modifiers. Specs in `generateResolveMeleeEvent.test.ts`: a blow of 5 turns a cavalry unit with Reverse 5, and does not once it commits Defence; its card adds nothing to the enemy's thresholds.

<a id="b4"></a>**B4. A rout forced by a blocked retreat cannot be paid.** _Closed: S11._

- **Rule:** 7.5 edge case and 9.1.6, a unit that must retreat and cannot routs and its owner pays Morale (9.3).
- **Code does:** the engine opens the rout (`triggerRoutFromRetreat`) and `findRoutLocation` can find it, but the rout sits inside the retreat and `updateRoutState` has no case for writing it. For an attack-apply or front-engagement parent it throws, so `applyChooseRoutDiscardEvent` cannot pay it. A zero penalty also closes the nested rout without closing its retreat, which `getExpectedRetreatEvent` then refuses. Marked `TODO(wrong)`. (`src/domain/transforms/pureTransforms/sequencing/updateRoutState.ts:26-37`; AUDIT.md `transforms`)
- **Probe:** a unit at the board edge hit to Retreat runs `openRetreat → triggerRoutFromRetreat → resolveRout → chooseRoutDiscard`; the discard throws "No rout state found in attack apply state" (`updateRoutState.ts:113`). With Morale 0, `getExpectedEvent` throws instead. The game stalls either way.
- **Closed:** `updateRoutState` now finds a rout held by a retreat and writes through `updateRetreatRoutState`, which closes the retreat when its rout completes, whether paid or a zero penalty. Specs: `applyChooseRoutDiscardEvent.test.ts`, `updateRetreatRoutState.test.ts`.

<a id="b5"></a>**B5. A card with two commit stats applies both.** _Settled: both at once; proved in S17._

- **Rule:** §8.2: committing gives +1 to every stat the card names, and both of a two-stat card's stats land at once (author, 2026-11-08: if too strong, rework later). The card may be committed at any moment where it can contribute **something**, and it contributes only what that moment uses — the spare stat is simply ignored, which §8.2 already prints.
- **Code does:** `modifiersFromCompletedCommitment` turns every stat on the card into +1, with no choice (`queries/card/modifiersFromCompletedCommitment.ts`; a card may name one or two, `entities/card/commandCard.ts`). No stand-in card names two, so it is untested in play.
- **To settle:** Nothing but proof: a spec with a two-stat card committed at a moment that uses one stat, and one that uses both.
- **Proved (S17):** `modifiersFromCompletedCommitment.test.ts` (a Range and Attack card gives +1 to both) and `getCurrentUnitStat.test.ts` (each stat takes its own +1; a stat the card does not name is untouched).

<a id="b6"></a>**B6. Flexibility and Range commitments are offered and never read.** _Range closed in S15; Flexibility waits for the deficit (A3)._

- **Rule:** §8.4 lists the stats each moment accepts. Flexibility counts before a move (once A8 is fixed). Range extends a shot's forward arc one space further out (§4.3.11, committed before the target is chosen). In a shot or a melee, +1 Flexibility shrinks a deficit (§2.5.2), so it matters only on rough ground.
- **Code does:** Commitments are read only in the front-engagement Speed contest, the ranged attack value and target thresholds, and the melee attack value (`procedures/movement/generateResolveEngageRetreatOptionEvent.ts`; `resolveAttack/`). The engine still accepts Range and Flexibility in shooting, and Flexibility in melee (`getLegalCommitTo*Events.ts`).
- **To settle:** Make the engine read +1 Range when it builds the forward arc, which means offering the commitment before the target is chosen: the attacker's one window, whose card counts for the whole attack (author, 2026-11-09). A Range commitment does not count when deciding whether a unit has a target at all (author, 2026-11-09: simplified for now), and it reaches the shooter only, not its supporters. Keep offering Flexibility in a shot and a melee; it starts to count when the deficit lands (A3).
- **Range, closed in S15:** the shooter commits before it aims, and `getLegalShooterTargets` builds the arc with that card's Range counted. Flexibility is still offered (to the shooter and, for its own thresholds, to the target) and read nowhere until the deficit lands (A3).

<a id="b7"></a>**B7. Both hands empty at once is a draw in the engine; the rule gives it to initiative.** _Closed 2026-11-08._

- **Rule:** §11.1: an empty hand loses at once, so playing or committing your last card loses. §11.3: if both hands empty at the same moment, the player who held initiative going into the round wins (author decision 2026-11-08).
- **Closed:** The engine now gives the tie to the holder of initiative — still the previous round's at that moment, since the new initiative is only assigned after the reveal — and the draw is gone from the whole chain: query, event schema, and `GameState.winner` (slice S1, `51c5521`). `getExpectedEvent` asks `getGameOverWinner` before every event, so playing or committing your last card ends the game at once, as the rule says.

<a id="b8"></a>**B8. No-units-left is not a loss.** _Closed 2026-11-08._

- **Rule:** §11.4: a player with no units loses. Confirmed as intended (author, 2026-11-08): it will almost never come up, but it is true.
- **Closed:** `getWinnerFromNoUnits` asks last, after the hand arms, in the rulebook's own order (slice S2). An army still in reserve has not been fielded, so setup never reads as a loss.

<a id="b9"></a>**B9. Melee results are carried out unit by unit; the rule is stage by stage.** _Closed: S12._

- **Rule:** §7.5.6: both retreats, then both reverses, then both routs, with the first player's blow landing first, so the second player's unit suffers first in each stage.
- **Code does:** The engine carries out the first player's unit's results in full (retreat, reverse, rout), then the second player's. A probe on 2026-11-08 confirmed that order; the stage order is read, not probed. The two differ when one unit's retreat would end the other's engagement before its reverse: a second-player unit that only reverses is judged before the first player's unit falls back, so it is still engaged and does not turn, where the rule has it turn.
- **Closed:** `getExpectedMeleeResolutionEvent` walks the stages (`attackApplyStages`) across both applies in the order the blows land (`getMeleeAppliesInResolutionOrder`: the second player's unit first), asking `getExpectedAttackApplyStageEvent` what each unit still needs; a ranged attack's single apply walks the same stages. The openers, the `getNextMelee*` selectors and `findNextMeleeDefendingPlayer` read the same order. The slots stay named for whose unit holds the result, so resolution order is the reverse of the slot names (author, 2026-11-09: the first player strikes first). Specs: `getExpectedMeleeResolutionEvent.test.ts`. Played end to end in `scenarios/meleeStages.test.ts`: a unit only hit to reverse turns once its enemy has fallen back; when both rout, the second player is asked for its Morale first, and if it cannot pay the game ends with the first player's unit still standing (§11.2). The stand-in units cannot be hit to reverse alone, so that scenario writes the measured results itself and lets the engines play on.

<a id="b10"></a>**B10. A rear engagement's Morale was never paid.** _Bug — found and closed 2026-11-09._

- **Rule:** §6.3.3: the defender is routed immediately and its owner pays its Morale at once; the engaging unit remains where it stands.
- **Code did:** The rear engagement routed the defender off the board the moment it was set up, and the movement sequencer then looked at the target space, saw no enemy, and completed the move — clearing the pending rout with it. Probe: after a rear attack on a Morale-2 unit, the defender's hand was still five cards. A rear attack cost the defender the unit and never the cards. Unnoticed because no spec played a rear engagement through from the move to the discard.
- **Closed:** The sequencer asks an engagement under way what comes next before it looks at the board (`getExpectedMovementResolutionEvent`). The attacker steps into the vacated space as the rout opens, so it stands where it fought before the Morale is asked for (author, 2026-11-09; `applyStartEngagementEvent`). Paying the discard, or a zero-Morale rout with nothing to pay, both end the engagement through one transform (`closeRearEngagementWhenRouted`). A scenario spec (`scenarios/rearEngagement.test.ts`) now plays the rule end to end, including §11.4 ending the game first when the defender was its side's last unit.

## Changes from Draft 3

These were errors in the earlier books, not in the engine. The engine's values are deliberate, and this book now prints them.

- **Army limits** (§3.1.3): cost cap 200 / 300 / 500 including cards, Morale floor 10 / 20 / 30, up to 12 copies of a type; Tutorial has no cost, Morale or spread limit (`ruleValues/ruleValues.ts`, `legality/army/`; set in `48aba02`). Draft 3's first-game armies were short of the Morale floor; Part I now fields 4 + 1.
- **Passing through a friend** (§5.4.2) needs combined Flexibility **6**, not 4 (`MIN_FLEXIBILITY_THRESHOLD`; `48aba02`). Roster Flexibility values were raised to match (§15).
- **Units, cards and traits** now come from the cycle 1 roster, not from Draft 3's Punic Wars table or the engine's stand-ins. The examples in Part I, §2.5.2, §7.2, §8 and §10.4 were reworked to match. Notably, two Manipular Legions can now pass through each other (Flexibility 3 + 3), and Punic Citizen Spearmen have Speed 1.
- **"The spaces behind"** a unit is the whole half of the board beyond its back rank, not a wedge (`queries/board/areas/getSpacesBehind.ts`) (§2.3.3).
- **Shooting into a melee** is forbidden (§4.3.11). Draft 3 was silent; the engine has required a lone target since `cd05333`. Confirmed as intended 2026-11-08.
- **An unresolved engagement is fought again next round** (§7.2.3). Confirmed by probe.
- **The support example** in the oldest rulebook (U at E-5 facing East, supporter at D-4) put the supporter in a back space, where it gives nothing (`queries/attack/getMeleeSupportValue.ts`). §7.3.2 replaces it.

## Engine findings with no rule at stake

These do not change how the game plays today, but they are worth the next read of the code.

- **Stand-in units and cards.** The engine's sample catalog (`sampleValues/tempUnits.ts`, `tempCommandCards.ts`, `miniStarterArmy.ts`) is test-fixture data, not the game's units. The authoritative units and cards live in version control and change faster than fixtures should. The cycle 1 roster is a more representative source for placeholder values: it would remove the Reverse-0 units, the Flexibility-0 elephants and the deck where nearly every range is 1. The trait list in `ruleValues/traits.ts` says `skirmisher` where the roster says `skirmish`.

- **Faults inside movement checks read as "illegal move".** `canEngageEnemy`, `canMoveInto` and `canMoveThrough` catch everything and return false (`legality/unitMovement/`; `TODO(wrong)` in each). `isLegalMove`, the fourth, is gone: a move's path is now judged by `traceMovePath`, which throws on a fault and leaves the catch to validation. `isValidLine` reports the fault instead, which is the shape the others should take.
- **Rally offered with an empty played pile, then throws** (`getLegalChooseRallyEvent.ts`, `generateResolveRallyEvent.ts`). Unreachable in play, because the round's card is always in the pile by then (probe-confirmed throw on a constructed state). Offer only "no rally" when the pile is empty. **Closed in S13:** `getLegalChooseRallyEvent` offers a rally only while the played pile holds a card to burn; validation follows it.
- **`getNextMeleeRoutState` returned a rout already paid** (closed in S13: it passes over a paid rout). It still has no production caller (`queries/sequencing/combatOutcomes/rout/`).
- **`getNextStepForResolveRally` defaults any non-resolve step to complete** (`queries/sequencing/rallyResolution/cleanupPhase/`; `TODO(wrong)`). Callers guard it today.
