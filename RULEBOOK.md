## Ancient Battles — Rulebook

_Draft 4 · for playtest_

---

### How to use this book

**Never played before?** Part I is all you need. It sets up your first battle and walks you through it. Twenty minutes, and it is easier with the pieces in front of you.

**Playing already?** Part II is the full ruleset, numbered so you can find and cite any rule. Part III has a one-page summary, a list of the situations that cause arguments, and the unit roster.

Throughout, a reference like §4.3.2 points to a numbered rule in Part II. You can ignore them on a first read.

This book states the game as designed. A small mark like means the digital edition does not yet play that rule as written, or that the rule itself is still open. The appendices at the back say what differs and what would settle it. At the table, play the rule as printed.

# PART I — LEARN TO PLAY

## 1. The short version

Two armies stand on a grid. You each command some troops and one general.

Every round, you both play a card face down and turn it over together. The card says what orders you are allowed to give. You give them. Units walk forward, bump into each other, and fight.

Fighting costs you cards.

**When a player's hand of cards runs out, that player loses.** Your hand is your army's will to fight; when it is gone, the battle is over.

That is the whole game. The rest is how it plays.

## 2. Set up your first battle

**The board.** Put it between you. It is wider than it is deep; you each sit at a long edge. It needs no markings — nothing in this game is described by naming a square.

**Your armies.** For a first game, use these two. Each is a legal Mini army with no Morale to spare.

| White — Rome     |     | Black — Carthage       |     |
| ---------------- | --- | ---------------------- | --- |
| Manipular Legion | ×4  | Punic Citizen Spearmen | ×4  |
| Equites          | ×1  | Punic Citizen Cavalry  | ×1  |
| **114 points**   |     | **109 points**         |     |

The points shown are for the units; your cards count toward the limit too (§3.1.3). For a mirror match, both of you can take the Roman list.

**Your cards.** Take eight command cards: two showing initiative 1, two showing 2, two showing 3, two showing 4. For a first game, prefer cards whose command says _Move_, and read the small print: a card that names a kind of unit you do not have cannot order anyone. These eight cards are your hand for the whole battle. There is no pile to draw from — what you hold is what you get, and the only way to get spent cards back is to rally, which you will meet in Phase 5.

**Deploy.** White places the whole army first, then Black. Place your units in the three rows nearest your own edge, one to a space. Stay clear of the two columns at each end — those are the open flanks, and nobody starts in them. Point every unit at the enemy. Black gets to see where White stands before choosing.

**Your general.** Put it anywhere in those same three rows. It can share a space with one of your units.

**Black has initiative** for the first round.

## 3. Your units

A unit is a whole formation of men — a legion, a squadron of horse, a screen of skirmishers. Not one soldier.

Four numbers tell you what it can do:

|                 |                                         |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| **Attack**      | how hard it hits                        |
| **Speed**       | how many spaces it can step in one move |
| **Flexibility** | how many times it can turn in one move  |
| **Range**       | how far it can shoot, if at all         |

Three more tell you what it takes to break it. These are **thresholds**: hit a unit hard enough to reach one, and that happens to it. Nothing carries over between blows. There is no damage to track, only where the unit now stands and which way it faces.

|             |                                          |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **Retreat** | it gives ground                          |
| **Reverse** | it is spun round to face the wrong way   |
| **Rout**    | it breaks and leaves the battle for good |

They are printed in that order. They usually climb, but not always: a unit's Retreat can be higher than its Reverse, and either can be as high as its Rout.

Finally, **Morale** is what a unit's rout costs you: discard that many cards from your hand. Cheap skirmishers cost nothing. Good troops hurt.

### Facing is the whole game

Every unit points one of eight ways, and you must always be able to tell which. This matters more than any number on the unit.

```
@@FIG facing-p1@@
        ↑  the way it is facing

     ·   ·   ·      ← its three front spaces
     ·  [U]  ·      ← its two flanking spaces
     ·   ·   ·      ← its three back spaces
```

Three things follow from it, and you will use all three in your first game:

- A unit **only ever walks forwards**. To go anywhere else, it turns first.
- A friendly unit helps you fight most when it is **looking at the fight**, and a little when the fight is off to its side.
- Being caught **from behind** does not give the enemy an advantage. It kills you outright.

## 4. Your cards

A card has no rules text. It is a set of printed values, each doing a different job. For your first game you need two of them.

**Initiative, 1 to 4.** Low goes first.

**The command.** What you may order this round — for example _4 units, Move_, or _2 lines, Shoot_. Most commands only reach units standing near your general.

Pick up the other three as you play:

- a **round effect**, a bonus that applies all round to your units near your general;
- **unit support**, which decides how large an army your hand can hold together;
- and a **stat it can be committed for**, letting you throw the card away for +1 at a critical moment.

## 5. The round

Five phases, in this order, every round.

|       | Phase         | Who goes first       |
| ----- | ------------- | -------------------- |
| **1** | Play cards    | both at once         |
| **2** | Move generals | first player         |
| **3** | Give orders   | first player         |
| **4** | Fight         | first player chooses |
| **5** | Clean up      | first player         |

### Phase 1 — Play cards

Both of you pick a card from hand and put it face down. Turn them over together.

**The lower initiative number wins initiative** for this round and goes first in every phase that follows. That makes you the **first player** for the round, and your opponent the **second player**. On a tie, whoever already had it keeps it.

Your card stays face up in front of you all round. You are committed to it.

### Phase 2 — Move generals

The first player goes first, then the second.

Your general moves up to **4 spaces**, any direction, and can walk over and stop on your own units. It cannot enter a space with an enemy unit in it. It can also stay where it is.

Generals never fight. They are there because most cards only reach units standing near them.

### Phase 3 — Give orders

First player gives **all** their orders and carries them out. Then the second player does the same.

**How many orders you get.** Read your card. _4 units_ means you may order up to four of your units — fewer is fine, and you may give no order at all. _2 lines_ means two orders, each aimed at a whole **line**: your units standing shoulder to shoulder in a row, all facing the same way (or exactly opposite), up to eight of them at once. A line is how you move an army instead of a unit.

A unit cannot be ordered twice in a round, and a unit already locked in a fight cannot move.<sup>[A1](#a1)</sup>

**Moving a unit.** Spend its two allowances in any order you like:

- **Speed** — one point per space stepped. A unit steps only in the direction it faces.
- **Flexibility** — one point per turn. A turn is 45° to either side, or a full about-face.<sup>[A2](#a2)</sup>

You cannot walk through an enemy, and you cannot stop on a friend. You _can_ walk through a friend if your Flexibility and theirs add up to 6 or more.

> _A Manipular Legion has Speed 2, Flexibility 2. It can step two spaces straight ahead. Or turn, step, turn, step. Or step, step, turn, turn. Any order works, so long as it spends no more than two of each. It cannot walk through another Legion either: 2 and 2 make only 4. An Equites (Flexibility 4) can slip through a Legion: 4 and 2 make 6._

**Walking into the enemy.** Step into a space with an enemy in it and you are **engaged**: you both stand in that one space until the fight resolves in Phase 4. What you get depends on which way the two of you end up facing.

| You are facing…               | What happens                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **opposite** the enemy        | Head on. If it is faster than you it may back off; otherwise you fight in Phase 4.             |
| **across** the enemy          | You hit its flank. It is dragged round to face you, cannot back off, and you fight in Phase 4. |
| **the same way** as the enemy | You came from behind. It routs on the spot. No fight at all.                                   |

That last row is why facing is the whole game.

> **Shooting.** If your card's command says _Shoot_ instead of _Move_, your ordered units with Range shoot rather than move. Each picks one enemy in the widening cone of spaces ahead of it. Several units ordered by the same card can gang up on one target: one shoots, the rest add +1 each instead of shooting themselves. Skip this on your first game if your cards let you.

### Phase 4 — Fight

Every engaged space fights. The first player picks the order.

Both units in a fight attack **at the same time**. Both can break. There is no attacker and defender.

For each of them, add up:

|                |                                              |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| its **Attack** | the number on the unit                       |
| **support**    | see below                                    |
| **+1**         | if that player throws away a card to help it |
| any bonus      | from its own card this round                 |

**Support** often tips a fight. Look at each friendly unit standing next to the fighter, as long as it is not behind it:

- it is **looking at the fight** → **+2**
- the fight is **off to its left or right** → **+1**
- it is looking away, or it is stuck in its own fight → **nothing**

Now take each unit's total and compare it against **the other unit's** three thresholds, left to right:

- reaches its **Retreat** → it gives ground
- reaches its **Reverse** → it is spun round
- reaches its **Rout** → it is removed, and its owner discards Morale cards

The results are carried out a stage at a time: both retreats, then both reverses, then both routs. In each stage the first player's unit goes first. A unit that is going to rout anyway can ignore its own retreat and reverse.

> **A worked fight.** _Your Legion (Attack 4) charges a Spearmen unit (Attack 3). The Spearmen are no faster than your Legion (both Speed 2), so they cannot back off and the two of you lock in. Two more of your Legions stand beside the fight: one is looking straight at it, one is side-on. That is +2 and +1, so your total is 4 + 3 = **7**. The Spearmen have nobody beside them: their total is **3**._
>
> _Spearmen thresholds are Retreat 6, Reverse 7, Rout 8. Your 7 reaches Retreat and Reverse but not Rout — so they give ground, then spin round. Badly placed now._
>
> _Your Legion's thresholds are Retreat 5, Reverse 6, Rout 8. Their 3 reaches none of them. Your Legion is untouched._
>
> _The two supporting Legions did nothing but stand in the right place facing the right way, and they doubled the blow._

### Phase 5 — Clean up

Put your face-up card onto your **played pile**. It is not in your hand any more.

Now each player, starting with the first player, decides whether to **rally**. Rallying is the only way to get cards back.

**If you rally:** take one card from your played pile at random and **burn** it — it is gone from the game for good. Take everything else back into your hand: the rest of the played pile, and everything you discarded.

Then check that your cards can still hold your army together. Every card can steady a certain number of units, and says which sort. Add all that up and give each unit on the board one place. Any unit you cannot find a place for **routs**, and you pay its Morale.

**If you do not rally:** nothing happens. Your cards stay where they are and nobody checks your units. You just have fewer cards next round.

That is the central squeeze of the game. You need your cards back, and getting them back costs one card forever and puts your army to the test.

## 6. Go and play

Start the next round. Play three or four before you worry about playing well.

When something comes up that this part does not answer — where exactly a retreating unit may go, whether a diagonal is blocked, how far a line can stretch — look in Part II under the obvious heading. Section 13 collects the questions that actually come up at the table, in the words people ask them.

# PART II — THE RULES

## 1. Golden rules

**1.1** Cards carry no rules text. Everything a card does is printed as values — an initiative number, a command, a round effect, a support grant, a commit stat — and this book governs how those values are read. A card never overrides a rule.

**1.2** If a rule says you _may_ do a thing, you may always decline. If it says you _must_, you must, and if you cannot, see **§11**.

**1.3** Where two effects would resolve at the same moment and the order matters, the **first player resolves first** (§4.1.5).

**1.4** All information on the board is open. Hands are secret. A card played face down in Phase 1 is secret until revealed. Committed cards are revealed as they are committed.

**1.5** Nothing is remembered between rounds except what is physically on the table: positions, facings, card piles, whose initiative it is, and any engagement that was not settled. There is no tracking of damage, fatigue, or previous orders.

## 2. The battlefield

### 2.1 The board

**2.1.1** The board is a rectangular grid of square spaces, wider than it is deep. The two players face each other across it from the long edges. Depth is measured between the players; frontage runs across.

**2.1.2 No notation is needed.** Positions in this book are always described relative to a unit — the space ahead of it, the space behind it, its flanking spaces — and never by a fixed name. An unmarked mat is enough to play on. A digital board or a printed one may label its rows and columns as a convenience, but no rule depends on those labels and you never have to read one out.

**2.1.3** Three board sizes exist. Each is given as **depth × frontage**:

| Board    | Size    | Spaces | Used by        |
| -------- | ------- | ------ | -------------- |
| Small    | 8 × 12  | 96     | Tutorial, Mini |
| Standard | 12 × 18 | 216    | Standard       |
| Large    | 24 × 36 | 864    | Epic           |

**2.1.4** Every space is bare, open, level ground. Nothing on the board slows a unit, hides it, or lifts it, and nothing stands between a shooter and its target.<sup>[A3](#a3)</sup>

### 2.2 Occupancy

**2.2.1** A space holds **at most one unit**, with one exception: two enemy units may occupy the same space while **engaged** (§6). Such a space is an **engaged space**.

**2.2.2** Commanders do not occupy space. A commander may share a space with a friendly unit, and more than one thing may stand in a space if one of them is a commander.<sup>[A4](#a4)</sup>

**2.2.3** No unit may enter or pass through an engaged space, for any reason, until that engagement is resolved. Neither may a commander.

### 2.3 Facing

**2.3.1** Every unit faces one of eight directions: toward any of the four sides of its space, or toward any of the four corners. Facing is open information (§1.4), and the whole game turns on it.

**2.3.2** Relative to a unit's facing, the eight surrounding spaces divide into three groups:

- **Front spaces** — three: the space it faces and the two to either side of that (45° off).
- **Flanking spaces** — two: directly to its left and right (90° off).
- **Back spaces** — three: the remaining spaces behind it.

```
@@FIG facing@@
        ↑  the way it is facing

     ·   ·   ·      ← its three front spaces
     ·  [U]  ·      ← its two flanking spaces
     ·   ·   ·      ← its three back spaces
```

**2.3.3 Spaces behind.** Beyond the three back spaces, the phrase _the spaces behind a unit_ means everything on the board on the far side of those three: the line through them, extended edge to edge, and every space beyond it. It is the whole back half of the field, not a narrow wedge. This matters for retreats (§9.1) and rear attacks (§6.2.2).

### 2.4 Adjacency, distance, and the forward arc

**2.4.1 Adjacent** means orthogonally or diagonally touching — up to eight spaces.

**2.4.2 Distance** is counted in steps where a diagonal step counts as **one**. "Within 3 spaces" means within three such steps. "Within 0 spaces" means the same space.

**2.4.3 The forward arc** is used for shooting (§7.4). Begin with the shooter's three front spaces; that is arc 1. For arc 2, add the three front spaces of each of those, and so on out to the shooter's Range. The result is a widening cone. The shooter's own space is not in its arc.

**2.4.4 Blocked diagonals.** Two enemy units standing in both of the orthogonal spaces that flank a diagonal **block** that diagonal. One enemy unit does not. A blocked diagonal cannot be stepped across (§5.4) and cannot carry support (§7.3.2).

```
@@FIG diagonal@@
   A diagonal step passes between the two
   spaces that flank the corner you cut.

   Enemies in BOTH of them:  blocked.
   An enemy in only one:     fine.
```

## 3. Armies and setup

### 3.1 Building an army

**3.1.1** An army is a list of units and a set of command cards, built to the limits of the chosen game mode.

**3.1.2** The cards you choose **are** your hand. You hold all of them from the first round. There is no pile to draw from, nothing in reserve, and no way to add a card once the battle has begun.

**3.1.3** Mode limits:

|                                      | Tutorial | Mini  | Standard | Epic  |
| ------------------------------------ | -------- | ----- | -------- | ----- |
| Board                                | Small    | Small | Standard | Large |
| Max total cost (units **and** cards) | no limit | 200   | 300      | 500   |
| Max different unit types             | 4        | 4     | 8        | 12    |
| **Minimum total Morale**             | none     | 10    | 20       | 30    |
| Cards of _each_ initiative value     | any      | 2     | 3        | 4     |
| Total cards in hand                  | any      | 8     | 12       | 16    |

Total cost adds up the cost of every unit you field, counting each copy, **and** the cost of every command card you bring. Total Morale adds up the Morale of every unit you field, counting each copy.

**3.1.4** Each unit type shows a **limit** — the most copies of it one army may field (never more than 12).

**3.1.5** No unit type may be listed twice, and no command card may appear twice in one army.

**3.1.6 The morale floor is a leash.** You must field at least the minimum total Morale, which means you cannot build an army of cheap, worthless troops that costs you nothing when it dies. Somebody in your list has to matter.

**3.1.7 The initiative spread is also a leash.** You must take an equal number of cards at each initiative value 1 through 4. No army can be built to always go first.

### 3.2 Deployment

**3.2.1** Decide which long edge each player takes. One is **White**, one is **Black**.

**3.2.2** Your **deployment zone** is the band of **three rows nearest your own edge**, less the **outermost two columns at each end**. The same rule gives the zone on every board size; only the width changes.

| Board    | Zone        | Frontage       |
| -------- | ----------- | -------------- |
| Small    | 3 rows deep | 8 spaces wide  |
| Standard | 3 rows deep | 14 spaces wide |
| Large    | 3 rows deep | 32 spaces wide |

The two columns at each end lie outside both deployment zones. They are the open flanks: nobody begins there, and getting into them is most of what fast troops are for.

**3.2.3** **White deploys first, then Black.** Each player places their whole army at once: every unit in their zone, one per space, each facing any direction they choose. Black has seen White's army before placing.<sup>[A5](#a5)</sup>

**3.2.4** Place your commander on any space in your zone. It may share that space with one of your own units.

**3.2.5** **Black holds initiative** going into round one. Play begins at Phase 1, where the cards may take it away again.

> **Designer's note.** Deployment is deliberately crude in this draft: a flat belt, both sides symmetrical, no scenario terrain, no screening rules, no hidden setup. Scenario-specific zones are the obvious next thing to build.

## 4. The round

A battle is fought in rounds. Each round has five phases, resolved in order, and no phase may be skipped — though a phase with nothing in it ends immediately.

### 4.1 Phase 1 — Play Cards

**4.1.1** Both players **simultaneously and secretly** choose one card from hand and place it face down.

**4.1.2** Reveal both cards together. Each is now **in play** and stays face up in front of its owner for the whole round.

**4.1.3** Compare initiative values. **The lower value takes initiative.**

**4.1.4** On a **tie**, initiative stays with whoever already had it.

**4.1.5** The player with initiative is the **first player** for the rest of the round and acts first in every phase that has an order. The other is the **second player**.

**4.1.6** From this moment until Cleanup your revealed card is doing two jobs: it grants its Command (§4.3) and it imposes its Round Effect (§4.3.7). It is no longer in your hand, so it cannot be committed (§8.6). Its unit support is unaffected — support is only ever measured after a rally, when every card you still own is back in your hand (§10.4).

**⚠ Edge case.** A card in play cannot be committed (§8). Once revealed, it is spoken for.

### 4.2 Phase 2 — Move Commanders

**4.2.1** The first player moves their commander, then the second player moves theirs.

**4.2.2** A commander may move up to **4 spaces**. Diagonal steps count as one.

**4.2.3** A commander has no facing and may change direction freely mid-move.

**4.2.4** A commander may enter and pass through any space **not containing an enemy unit** — including empty spaces and spaces holding friendly units. It may not enter a space holding an enemy unit, and therefore may not enter an engaged space.

**4.2.5** A commander may not cross a diagonal blocked by enemies (§2.4.4).

**4.2.6** A commander may stay where it is.

**4.2.7 Why this matters.** A card's command and its round effect each may carry an **inspiration range**, measured from your commander. A printed range of _none_ means no restriction. A printed range of 0 means the same space as your commander. Where a range is printed, your general's position decides which of your troops it reaches.<sup>[A6](#a6)</sup>

**4.2.8 Commander defeat.** A commander is defeated if it stands in a space where a unit routs in combat. A unit that routs at the support check (§10.4) does not defeat it.<sup>[A15](#a15)</sup> What defeat costs the player is not yet written (see Open design questions). A commander absent from the board reaches no unit with an inspiration range.

### 4.3 Phase 3 — Issue Commands

Your card says what you are allowed to order this round and how much of it. It might be _four units_, or it might be _two lines_ — a line being a whole rank of troops moving as one. You spend that allowance on the units you want to act, then you carry the orders out one by one.

The allowance is the interesting part. A card that orders one line can move eight units if they happen to be dressed in a rank; if your army is scattered, the same card moves one, since a lone unit is a line of one. Formation is not decoration here — it is how much you get to do.

This phase has four steps: the first player **issues** all their commands, then **resolves** all of them; then the second player does the same.

#### Issuing

**4.3.1** Your in-play card shows exactly one Command, with a **type** (Move or Shoot), a **size** and a **number**.

**4.3.2 Size: units.** You make **one** order, selecting **between one and the printed number** of eligible units. The number is a ceiling, not a quota. You may instead decline to order at all.

**4.3.3 Size: lines.** You make that many **separate** orders, each one aimed at a single **line** (§4.3.5). Two lines means two orders, resolved independently. You may stop after fewer. Whatever you do not issue is forfeited, and if none of your remaining orders can be given to anyone, you must stop.

**4.3.4 Eligibility.** A unit may be ordered only if it satisfies _all_ of the Command's restrictions, and has not already been ordered this round (§4.3.6):

- **Trait restrictions** — the unit must have **every** listed trait.
- **Unit restrictions** — the unit must be one of the listed types.
- **Inspiration range** — where one is printed, the unit must be within that many spaces of **your own commander** (§2.4.2), measured when you issue the order.

An **engaged** unit cannot be ordered. It is locked in its fight until the engagement ends (§6.1.2).<sup>[A1](#a1)</sup>

**4.3.5 Lines.** A **line** is a rank of your own units standing shoulder to shoulder:

- Every unit lies in the **flanking space** of the one beside it — that is, the line runs _across_ their facing, not along it.
- Every unit faces the **same direction, or exactly the opposite** direction as the unit you name first. A rank that has about-faced is still a rank.
- The line is **unbroken**: no gaps, no enemies, no non-qualifying units in the middle.
- A line is **1 to 8 units** long.

You select a line by naming its two ends. The **near end you name must satisfy the full restrictions** including inspiration range; the **rest of the line need only satisfy the trait and type restrictions**. A line therefore reaches beyond your general's voice, so long as it starts within it.

If an unbroken rank is longer than eight units, you choose which eight.<sup>[A7](#a7)</sup>

**An engaged unit in a rank** does not break the line. The line runs past it, but the engaged unit is not itself commanded: it stays locked in its fight and gets none of the Command's modifiers. That is intended, even though it can look inconsistent.<sup>[A1](#a1)</sup>

**4.3.6 One order per unit.** A unit may be commanded only **once per round**, whether as a single unit or as part of a line. Already-commanded units cannot be included in a later line, and a line cannot be drawn _through_ one — it stops there.

**4.3.7 Round Effects are not orders.** A card's Round Effect applies to every one of your units that meets its restrictions, all round, commanded or not. Its restrictions are read separately from the Command's and are usually different — in particular, a round effect's inspiration range is checked again whenever its bonus matters, wherever the unit is standing at that moment, not only when orders are given.<sup>[A6](#a6)</sup>

**4.3.8 Command modifiers apply only to the commanded.** A Command's printed modifiers (e.g. _+1 Attack, −2 Speed_) apply for the whole round, but **only to units that actually received that order**. They stay in force through Phase 4 — a unit ordered to charge still has its bonus when the melee resolves.

#### Resolving

**4.3.9** Resolve your issued commands one at a time, in any order you like, finishing each unit before beginning the next.

**4.3.10 Resolving a movement order** (§5):

1. Choose a unit that has not yet acted.
2. You may **commit** a card for **+1 Speed** or **+1 Flexibility** (§8). Optional.<sup>[A8](#a8)</sup>
3. Move the unit (§5).
4. If it moved into an enemy-occupied space, an **engagement** begins and is set up now (§6).

Your commander stays where it is; it moves only in Phase 2.<sup>[A9](#a9)</sup>

**4.3.11 Resolving a ranged attack order** (§7.4):

1. Choose the firing unit. It must be unengaged and have Range of at least 1. A unit with no legal target, even counting a +1 Range commitment (step 2), is skipped. A Range commitment may therefore be what gives a unit its target.
2. Before choosing a target, the **attacker** may commit a card for **+1 Range**, which widens the forward arc by one space (§8, §2.4.3). Optional.<sup>[B7](#b7)</sup>
3. Choose one enemy target within its forward arc (§2.4.3). You cannot shoot into a melee: a unit in an engaged space is never a target.
4. Optionally name **supporters** — other units you ordered with this same command that have not yet fired, and that could each independently shoot that same target. Each supporter adds **+1**, and naming it spends that unit's own shot (§7.3.4).
5. The **attacker** may commit a card for **+1 Attack** (§8). Optional.
6. The **defender** may then commit a card for **+1 Defence** (§8.5). Optional.<sup>[B1](#b1)</sup>
7. Resolve the attack (§7).

A card naming Flexibility adds nothing to a shot until the terrain rules land (§8.4).

**⚠ Edge case.** An engaged unit can neither shoot nor support a shot. It is busy.

**⚠ Edge case.** Shooting is never automatic. Units with Range do nothing at all unless a Shoot command orders them to fire.

**⚠ Edge case.** **Line of sight is not yet part of the game.** Nothing blocks a shot: intervening units, friendly or enemy, are ignored, and if the target is in the arc and within Range it can be hit. Line of sight is planned, as terrain is; until it arrives, play it as written here.<sup>[A3](#a3)</sup>

### 4.4 Phase 4 — Resolve Melee

**4.4.1** Every engaged space on the board fights this phase. There is no choice about _whether_.

**4.4.2** The **first player** chooses which engagement to resolve, resolves it completely, then chooses the next, until none remain. The choice of order is entirely theirs.

**4.4.3** Resolve each engagement per §7.2.

**4.4.4** If there are no engagements, the phase ends immediately.

### 4.5 Phase 5 — Cleanup

**4.5.1 Discard played cards.** Both players move their in-play card to their **played pile**. It is not in your hand and it is not in your discard; it sits in the played pile until you rally or the game ends.

**4.5.2 The first player decides whether to rally**, and fully resolves that decision (§10.3) including the support check and any routs it causes.

**4.5.3 Then the second player** does the same.

**4.5.4** The round ends. A new round begins at Phase 1.

## 5. Movement

A unit walks forwards. That is the only thing it can do. Everything else about movement follows from that one restriction: to go anywhere other than straight ahead, a unit must first turn to face the way it wants to go, and turning is a separate, limited resource from walking.

A move is therefore a short sequence of steps and turns, in whatever order you like, paid for out of two allowances that refresh each time the unit is ordered.

### 5.1 The two resources

**5.1.1** A move spends two things: **Speed**, for stepping, and **Flexibility**, for turning. Both refresh every time the unit is ordered — they are not a pool that depletes over the battle. Both are the unit's **current** values: printed value plus any round effect that reaches it and any modifier from the command it was given, and any card committed for it (§4.3.7, §4.3.8, §8).

**5.1.2** Each step into an adjacent space costs **1 Speed**. Diagonal steps cost 1, same as orthogonal.

**5.1.3** A unit only ever steps **in the direction it is currently facing**. To go somewhere else, turn first.

### 5.2 Turning

**5.2.1** A facing change costs **1 Flexibility** and turns the unit either **45° to one side** or **180° about**. Wheeling 90° takes two turns, and so two Flexibility.<sup>[A2](#a2)</sup>

**5.2.2** Turns and steps may come in **any order**, and a unit may turn several times in a row. The only limit is the Speed and Flexibility it has to spend.

**5.2.3** A unit with Flexibility 0 cannot turn at all. It moves in a straight line or not at all.

### 5.3 What you may move into

A unit may **end its move** in a space that is:

- **its own starting space** (it did not move, or it turned on the spot), or
- **empty**, or
- **occupied by a single enemy unit**, if it may legally engage it (§6.2).

A unit may **never** end its move in a space occupied by another friendly unit, nor in an engaged space.

### 5.4 What you may move through

**5.4.1** Empty spaces: freely.

**5.4.2** Spaces with a **friendly** unit: only if **your Flexibility plus theirs is 6 or more**, using current values including all modifiers. Two stiff formations cannot interpenetrate; nimble troops can filter through.

**5.4.3** Spaces with an **enemy** unit: never.

**5.4.4** **Engaged spaces**: never.

**5.4.5 Diagonal steps** additionally require that at least **one** of the two orthogonal spaces flanking the diagonal be one you could pass through under 5.4.1 to 5.4.4. You cannot slip between two units standing corner to corner, and you cannot cross a diagonal blocked by enemies (§2.4.4). The step still costs only 1 Speed. The test looks only at those two corner spaces, never at the space you step into. A diagonal step that ends on a single enemy unit is an engagement, and it follows this same test and the engagement rules (§6.2) with no special case for being diagonal: enemies in both corner spaces stop you, an enemy in only one does not.<sup>[A10](#a10)</sup><sup>[A12](#a12)</sup>

**⚠ Edge case.** If you pass through several friendly units in one move, check the combined-Flexibility test **separately for each one**.

**⚠ Edge case.** A unit may be ordered and then not move at all — a legal move of zero steps and zero turns. It still counts as commanded, and still receives the Command's modifiers (§4.3.8). Ordering a unit to stand still and gain +1 Attack is a real and often correct play.

## 6. Engagements

Units do not stand next to each other and trade blows across a boundary. To fight, one unit walks into the other's space, and the two of them share it until the matter is settled. That shared space is an engagement.

Two questions follow, and the rest of this section answers them. _May I walk in at all?_ — which depends on the side I approach from. _And what kind of fight is it?_ — which depends on which way the two of us end up facing. Coming at a unit from behind does not merely give you an advantage in the fight; it means there is no fight.

### 6.1 What an engagement is

**6.1.1** An **engagement** is created the moment a unit ends a step in a space occupied by a single enemy unit. Both units now stand in that one space. It is an **engaged space**.<sup>[A11](#a11)</sup>

**6.1.2** Engagements are created in Phase 3 and fought in Phase 4. Between those moments the two units are locked together: neither can move, neither can shoot, neither can support anything, and nothing may enter or cross their space. A fight that neither side wins outright goes on into the next round (§7.2.3); the lock holds until it ends.

**6.1.3** The unit that moved in is the **engaging unit**. The unit that was already there is the **defender**.

### 6.2 When you may engage

Look at which of the defender's spaces you are stepping **from**:

**6.2.1 From a flanking space:** always allowed, whether the last step is straight or diagonal. A diagonal last step passes the same corner test as any other diagonal step (§5.4.5).<sup>[A12](#a12)</sup>

**6.2.2 From a back space:** allowed **only if your unit began this entire move in the region behind the defender** (§2.3.3). You cannot loop around a formation's flank and take it in the back in the same order. You must already have been behind it.

**6.2.3 From a front space:** allowed only if your unit **ends its move facing directly opposite the defender** — squared up, head on. If it is not already facing that way, it spends 1 Flexibility to turn as it closes, and without that Flexibility it cannot engage from there.<sup>[A13](#a13)</sup>

### 6.3 What kind of engagement it is

**6.3.1** Compare the **engaging unit's final facing** with the **defender's facing**. Note that this is facing against facing, not position.

| Engaging unit's facing, relative to defender's | Type      |
| ---------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| The same, or 45° off either way                | **Rear**  |
| 90° off either way                             | **Flank** |
| Directly opposite                              | **Front** |

Read it as a picture: if the two units are looking at each other, that is a Front engagement. If one is looking across the other, that is a Flank. If they are both looking the same way, the attacker has come from behind. Because a front approach always squares up (§6.2.3), no engagement falls between these three.<sup>[A13](#a13)</sup>

**6.3.2 Front engagement.** The defender may be offered the chance to fall back:

1. The defender may **commit** a card for +1 Speed or +1 Flexibility (§8). Optional. (The engaging player had their chance before moving, §4.3.10.)
2. Compare current **Speed**: each unit's full Speed, including round effects and command modifiers, not what is left of its move. The defender may retreat **only if its Speed is strictly greater** than the engaging unit's.<sup>[A11](#a11)</sup>
3. If it may and chooses to, it makes a retreat move (§9.1). The engagement ends at once; no melee is fought.
4. Otherwise both units stay locked and fight in Phase 4.

**6.3.3 Rear engagement.** The defender is **routed immediately** (§9.3). No melee, no commitment, no reply. Its owner pays its Morale at once. The engaging unit remains where it stands.

**6.3.4 Flank engagement.** The defender is **forced to rotate** to face the engaging unit — its new facing is directly opposite the engaging unit's. It may not retreat. The two fight in Phase 4.

A flank attack does no damage by itself. What it does is remove the defender's options: it cannot fall back, and it enters the melee having just been turned, which usually costs it the support of the units beside it (§7.3.1).

**⚠ Edge case.** A defender that is routed by a rear engagement pays Morale immediately, in Phase 3 — not in Phase 4, and not at Cleanup.

**⚠ Edge case.** If several of your units engage in one phase, each engagement is fully set up as its movement resolves. A unit that has already been engaged by one of your units cannot be engaged again — its space is now an engaged space and nothing may enter it (§2.2.3). You get one attacker per defender.

## 7. Combat

All combat, melee or ranged, works the same way: build an attack value, compare it to the target's three thresholds, apply what it reaches.

### 7.1 Attack value

**7.1.1** Start with the unit's printed **Attack**.

**7.1.2** Add every modifier that applies. They are simply summed, so the order does not matter:

1. **Round Effect** of the attacker's own in-play card, if the unit meets its restrictions (§4.3.7).
2. **Command modifiers** of that same card, if the unit was commanded this round (§4.3.8).
3. **Committed card**, if one was committed for this combat (§8).

**7.1.3** Then add **support** (§7.3).

**7.1.4** The result is the attack value. Modifiers may be negative, and an attack value cannot be reduced below zero.<sup>[B2](#b2)</sup>

**7.1.5** The same three sources can change a unit's **thresholds**. A Round Effect or Command modifier that names Defence raises Retreat, Reverse and Rout together, for as long as the card is in play. The same inspiration range and unit restrictions apply as for any Round Effect.

### 7.2 Melee

**7.2.1** Melee is **mutual and simultaneous**. Both units attack. Both may break. There is no attacker and defender in a melee, only two units in the same space.

**7.2.2** Procedure for one engagement:

1. The **first player** may commit a card for their unit. Optional.
2. The **second player** may then do the same. Optional.
3. Compute both attack values (§7.1), each including that side's own melee support. Support is counted on the board as it stands now, before either unit is touched.
4. Compare each unit's attack value against the **other** unit's thresholds.<sup>[B3](#b3)</sup>
5. Apply the results a stage at a time: both retreats, then both reverses, then both routs. In each stage the **first player's unit goes first** (§7.5.6).

A card naming Flexibility adds nothing to a melee until the terrain rules land (§8.4).

**7.2.3** If neither unit routs or retreats, they remain engaged and will fight again next round.

**7.2.4** In Phase 4 the first player chooses which engagement to fight next, until none are left (§4.4).

> _Attacker: Attack 4, +1 from its card's Round Effect, +3 support = **8**._
> _Defender: Attack 4, +2 support = **6**._
> _Attacker's thresholds — Retreat 5, Reverse 6, Rout 8. Defender's — Retreat 5, Reverse 6, Rout 7._
> _8 meets the defender's Rout: the defender breaks._
> _6 meets the attacker's Retreat and Reverse but not its Rout: the attacker falls back, then turns about._

> _A real pairing. A **Manipular Legion** (Attack 4; thresholds 5 / 6 / 8) with +3 support strikes **Libyan Spearmen** (Attack 4; thresholds 6 / 7 / 8) with +2 support. The Legion's value is 7, the Spearmen's is 6._
> _7 meets the Spearmen's Retreat (6) and Reverse (7), but not Rout (8). 6 meets the Legion's Retreat (5) and Reverse (6), but not Rout (8). Both units fall back and turn about. Nobody is removed, and nobody pays Morale._

### 7.3 Support

A unit does not fight alone. The friends standing around it add to its blow — and how much they add depends on whether they are actually facing the fight or looking somewhere else.

Support is typically +1 or +2, about the size of any other modifier. A unit wrapped on several sides can gather far more, but a full envelopment like that is rarely feasible. It is also a number you have a lot of control over, because it is decided entirely by where your units stand and which way you have pointed them. Melee and ranged support work differently and are described separately below.

(Do not confuse this with **unit support**, the cards-hold-units rule of §10.4. They share a word and nothing else.)

#### 7.3.1 Melee support

A friendly unit backs up your fighting unit in melee when it is in a position to lend a hand. All of these must be true:

- it stands **right beside** the fighter, diagonals included;
- it is **not behind** the fighter, meaning not in one of the fighter's back spaces;
- it is **free**: a unit locked in a fight of its own lends nothing;
- if it stands on a diagonal, the way between the two is **open** (§2.4.4). It is shut only when **both** of the two spaces at the corners between them hold enemy units. One enemy there does not shut it.

How much it lends depends on **where it is looking**, which is to say where the fighter lies from the supporter's point of view:

| The fighter is in the supporter's…  | Support |
| ----------------------------------- | ------- |
| **front spaces** (looking at it)    | **+2**  |
| **flanking spaces** (off to a side) | **+1**  |
| **back spaces** (looking away)      | none    |

**7.3.2** Add up every qualifying supporter. There is no cap, but the total stays modest in practice. One or two supporters, +1 or +2, is typical. +4, two strong supporters, is a total envelopment.

```
@@FIG support@@
      [S1]  [S2]
         \  /
         [ U ]  ──▶ engaged here

   S1 faces toward U      → +2
   S2 faces across U      → +1
   U fights at Attack + 3
```

> _Check the geometry before counting. U faces North, so its back spaces are the three behind it. S1 stands diagonally ahead of U on one side, turned toward it: +2. S2 stands diagonally ahead on the other side, turned so that U lies off its shoulder: +1. A third friend directly behind U adds nothing, however it faces._

**7.3.3** Support is recalculated fresh for each combat, from the board as it stands at that moment. Units that routed earlier in the phase are simply not there any more. This is why the first player's choice of which fight to resolve first matters (§4.4.2): clearing a unit away can change the support in every fight after it.

#### 7.3.4 Ranged support

Ranged support is much simpler and quite different from melee support. Each supporting unit adds **+1**, flat, regardless of facing or position.

A supporter must be one of your units that

- was **ordered by this same Shoot command**,
- has **not yet fired** its own shot this phase, and
- could **independently make that same shot** — unengaged, Range 1 or more, the target within its own forward arc.

Naming a unit as a supporter **spends its shot**. A command that orders four archers may therefore be fired as four separate attacks, or as a single attack at +3, or anything in between. Concentrating fire is how you break a hard target; spreading it is how you cover a frontage.

### 7.4 Ranged attacks

**7.4.1** The firing unit must be **unengaged** and have current **Range** of at least 1.

**7.4.2** The target must be an **unengaged** enemy unit in the firer's **forward arc** (§2.4.3) out to its current Range. A unit locked in an engagement cannot be shot. Nothing else blocks the shot; line of sight is not yet in the game (§4.3.11).<sup>[A3](#a3)</sup>

**7.4.3** Attack value = the firer's Attack (with its modifiers, §7.1) + 1 per supporter (§7.3.4).

**7.4.4** Ranged fire is **one way**. The target does not shoot back.

**7.4.5** The attacker commits first, for Range before choosing the target and for Attack after; then the defender, for Defence, raising the target's thresholds against this shot (§8.5).<sup>[B1](#b1)</sup>

### 7.5 Applying the result

**7.5.1** Compare the attack value against each of the target's three thresholds. A threshold is met if the attack value is **equal to or greater than** it. The three are printed on the unit in the order they resolve, left to right:

| Threshold met | Result                                                                                    |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Retreat**   | The unit falls back (§9.1).                                                               |
| **Reverse**   | The unit is turned 180° on the spot (§9.2).                                               |
| **Rout**      | The unit is removed from the battle; its owner discards cards equal to its Morale (§9.3). |

A printed threshold of **0** is met by any attack at all, even an attack of zero.<sup>[B4](#b4)</sup>

**7.5.2** An attack will often meet more than one threshold. A unit's results come in this order: **retreat, then reverse, then rout.** In a melee the two units' results are interleaved (§7.5.6).

**7.5.3 A rout saves you the trouble.** If the attack reaches the Rout threshold, the unit is leaving the board, so whether it would also have fallen back or turned about changes nothing for that unit. It may ignore its own retreat and reverse. Its rout still comes up in its place in the order (§7.5.6).

**7.5.4** A unit that retreats and reverses does both, in that order: it falls back, then turns about. Falling back first is often what makes the reverse possible at all — see 7.5.5.

**7.5.5** **A unit that is still sharing a space with an enemy does not reverse.** The reverse is checked at the moment it comes due. If the unit is still engaged then, it simply stays as it is. Within an engagement the two units are in the same situation and are suffering similar fatigue, so neither is yet worse off than the other. What the blow did to a unit shows on the battlefield once the engagement is resolved. A unit whose enemy has since fallen back or been removed is no longer engaged, so it does reverse, and that includes a unit that has just driven its enemy back.

**7.5.6** In a melee, both units' results are decided from the same two attack values, then **carried out a stage at a time: both retreats, then both reverses, then both routs.** In each stage the **first player's unit goes first**. The full order is first player's retreat, second player's retreat, first player's reverse, second player's reverse, first player's rout, second player's rout. Alternating at every step is how initiative counts in a melee: the first player's blow takes effect first at each one. This matters in three places. A unit that has already fallen back is no longer there to keep the other engaged, so the other can reverse (§7.5.5). A unit that is going to rout is still on the board until the rout stage, so the unit engaged with it is still engaged at the reverse stage and does not reverse. And if the first player's Morale payment is more than its owner can bear, the game ends there (§11.2), before the second unit's rout is carried out.

**⚠ Edge case.** A unit that **must** retreat but **has no legal retreat** (§9.1.5) **routs instead** — the one case where a result early in the order escalates into the last one. It is among the most dangerous situations in the game, and the reason a unit's back should never be against something it cannot cross.<sup>[B5](#b5)</sup>

## 8. Commitments

A commitment is how you lean on a unit in a moment that matters. Throw a card out of your hand and that unit does a little better than it should have: one point of attack, of defence, of speed, of flexibility, of range — whichever the card names.

It is always optional, it is always worth exactly +1, and the card comes back at your next rally. A committed card is lent, not spent: until you rally, you cannot play it or commit it again.

**8.1** A **commitment** is discarding one card from your hand to squeeze +1 out of a unit for one action. Committed cards go to your **discard pile** and come back when you rally (§10.3).

**8.2** A card names the stat it may be committed for — normally exactly one, and no card names more than two. Committing gives **+1** to every stat the card names. The value is never anything but +1. Only stats the moment actually uses can matter, so a second stat on the card is simply ignored where it is no use.<sup>[B6](#b6)</sup>

**8.3** Commitment is **always optional** and always declinable, including when your opponent has already committed. Committing your last card empties your hand and loses the game (§11.1).

**8.4** Each moment accepts only certain stats, so a card is committable there only if it names at least one of them. A card whose only stat is Range is no use in a melee, and is not offered. Flexibility does nothing in a shot or a melee until the terrain rules land (§A3), so a card whose only stat is Flexibility adds nothing there either.<sup>[B7](#b7)</sup>

| Moment                                     | Stats it accepts                               | Who commits first             |
| ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Before a movement order resolves (§4.3.10) | Speed, Flexibility                             | The moving player             |
| Defending a front engagement (§6.3.2)      | Speed, Flexibility                             | The defender                  |
| A ranged attack (§4.3.11)                  | Attack or Range (attacker), Defence (defender) | Attacker, then defender       |
| A melee (§7.2.2)                           | Attack, Defence                                | First player, then the second |

**8.4.1** A commitment helps **your own unit only**. In a front engagement, the mover's Speed commitment (made before it moved) and the defender's are each added to their own unit before the two Speeds are compared (§6.3.2).

**8.5 Defence** as a commitment stat raises all three of a unit's thresholds by 1 for that combat, making it harder to make that unit retreat, reverse, or rout.<sup>[B3](#b3)</sup>

**8.6** A card in play (§4.1.6) cannot be committed. Only cards in hand can.

> _Example. Your hand holds **Brace** (commit stat: Defence) and **Fearless Velites** (commit stat: Attack). In a melee you commit Fearless Velites: +1 Attack for your unit. Next round you commit Brace instead. Both cards sit in your discard pile until you rally._

**⚠ Edge case.** Commitments come back on your next rally, so a committed card is lent, not spent. What it costs you is the interval: until you rally, that card cannot be played, cannot be committed again, and cannot break a tie for initiative.

## 9. Retreat, reverse, rout

The three things an attack can do to a unit, in the order they are carried out (§7.5.2): retreat, then reverse, then rout. A unit that is going to rout can ignore the other two.

### 9.1 Retreat

**9.1.1** A retreat is a **backward move**, made with the unit's own Speed and Flexibility.

**9.1.2** A retreating unit must stay within **the region behind its starting position** (§2.3.3) — both the spaces it passes through and the space it ends in. It may not end where it started.

**9.1.3** A retreat must be the **smallest legal backward move available**: the fewest facing changes first, then the fewest steps. You do not get to use a retreat as a free repositioning. Where several moves tie for smallest, the owner chooses among them. Speed and Flexibility are the unit's current ones, with any Round Effect or Command modifiers (§7.1.5).

**9.1.4** Before its first step back, a retreating unit may turn by **at most 45°** in all. It may not turn about, so a retreat never begins by facing the other way.<sup>[A14](#a14)</sup>

**9.1.5** A unit **cannot retreat at all** if:

- an **unengaged enemy unit stands in one of its back spaces, facing it** — its line of withdrawal is cut; or
- no legal backward move exists — the board edge, friendly units it cannot pass, or enemies are in the way.

**9.1.6** A unit that is required to retreat and cannot, **routs** (§7.5, edge case).<sup>[B5](#b5)</sup>

**9.1.7** A retreating unit may not enter an enemy-occupied space. A retreat never creates an engagement.

### 9.2 Reverse

A reverse stands for a unit that has been worn down, thrown into disorder or shaken, and is left in a vulnerable position until it is shored up. It is shown by turning the unit round because that works well in this system. It is not an about-face in meaning.

**9.2.1** The unit turns 180° where it stands. Nothing else changes.

**9.2.2** A reverse costs no Flexibility and is not a move.

**9.2.3** It cannot be applied to a unit still engaged (§7.5.5).

**9.2.4** Reverse is the mildest result on paper and often not in practice. A reversed unit's front becomes its back, so the enemy that just struck it is now standing behind it, and a rear engagement next round routs it outright (§6.3.3).

### 9.3 Rout

**9.3.1** A routed unit is **removed from the battle permanently**. It does not come back.

**9.3.2** Its owner immediately discards cards from hand equal to that unit's **Morale**, chosen freely from their hand. Morale 0 costs nothing.

**9.3.3** If several units of one player rout at the same moment — as happens at the support check (§10.4) — sum their Morale and discard that many at once. In a melee the two sides are separate: each owner pays for their own unit, the first player first.

**9.3.4** If you cannot pay, you lose (§11.2).

**9.3.5 Commander casualty.** A commander standing in the space of a unit that routs in combat is defeated with it (§4.2.8). A unit that routs at the support check (§10.4) does not defeat it.<sup>[A15](#a15)</sup>

## 10. Cards: hand, piles, rally, support

There is one set of cards and it does everything: it is your hand, your losses, your recovery, and the measure of how much army you can keep on the table. Cards move between a few places, and only one of those moves is permanent.

Cards leave your hand three ways — played, committed, or discarded to pay for a rout. All three come back when you rally. Rallying destroys one card, at random, forever. That single burnt card per rally is the only permanent loss in the whole system, and it is the reason a long battle grinds an army down.

### 10.1 The five places a card can be

| Place                                              | In your hand? | Comes back?                   |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Hand**                                           | yes           | —                             |
| **In play** — this round's card                    | no            | to the played pile at Cleanup |
| **Played pile** — cards played in earlier rounds   | no            | on rally, minus one burnt     |
| **Discard pile** — commitments and Morale payments | no            | on rally, all of it           |
| **Burnt**                                          | no            | **never**                     |

**10.1.1** Only cards **in your hand** can be played or committed.

**10.1.2** Unit support, by contrast, does not depend on where a card was. The support check happens only in the instant after a rally (§10.4), and a rally has just gathered every card you still own into your hand. So a card provides its support unless it has been **burnt**. Burning is the only thing that takes support away.

### 10.2 How cards leave your hand

- **Played** in Phase 1 → in play → played pile.
- **Committed** for a bonus (§8) → discard pile.
- **Discarded to pay Morale** when a unit routs (§9.3) → discard pile.

A card is never burnt from your hand. The card burnt at a rally comes from your played pile (§10.3), and is gone for good.

### 10.3 Rallying

**10.3.1** In Cleanup, after this round's card has gone to the played pile, each player in turn, starting with the first player, may choose to rally. It is the **only** way to get cards back into your hand.

**10.3.2** To rally:

1. **Burn** one of your played cards, chosen at random. It is out of the game for good.
2. Take every other card in your played pile back into your hand.
3. Take your **entire discard pile** back into your hand.
4. Immediately perform the **unit support check** (§10.4).

**10.3.3** There is always something to burn: this round's card went to the played pile a moment ago (§4.5.1).

**10.3.4** If you decline to rally, nothing happens: your played pile keeps growing and **no support check occurs**. Units stay on the board regardless of whether your hand could hold them.

**10.3.5** Rallying costs you three things: one card destroyed for good, no say in which card that is, and a support check you may not be ready for. Against that, it is the only way to get your hand back, so the question is when, not whether.

**10.3.6** The first player finishes their rally entirely, support check and Morale payment included, before the second player decides whether to rally.

### 10.4 The unit support check

Your cards are what holds your army on the field. Each card can keep a certain number of units in good order — some cards will steady anything, most are particular about what they steady. When you rally, you check whether your cards can still account for everything you have on the board. Whatever they cannot account for breaks and leaves.

So it is a counting exercise, done once, in one go: _this is what my cards can hold, these are the units I have, anything left over is gone._

Three things make it less alarming than it sounds. It only happens when you rally, so you choose when to face it. Every card you own is in your hand at that moment, so nothing is missing from the count. And the number never drifts downward on its own — the only thing that shrinks it is burning, one card per rally.

#### The check in detail

**10.4.1 When.** Immediately after a rally, and only for the player who rallied (§10.3.2). At no other time.

**10.4.2 What a card holds.** Each card names one kind of unit it can steady, and how many:

- a **named unit type** — _Manipular Legion ×2_
- a **trait** — _phalanx ×1_
- **any unit at all** — _generic ×1_

A card always holds at least one place, so every card in your hand contributes.

**10.4.3 Add them up into pools.** Cards naming the same thing combine. Two cards each holding _Velites ×2_ make a single pool of _Velites ×4_. Cards naming different things stay separate: a _phalanx_ pool and a _generic_ pool are two pools, not one.

**10.4.4 Assign your units to the pools.** You do this yourself: for each pool, name which of your units fill its places. Every unit on your board needs a place in one pool. A unit may take **only one** place, and only in a pool it matches — a Libyan Spearman can go in a _Libyan Spearmen_ pool, a _phalanx_ pool, or a _generic_ pool, but into exactly one of them. A pool need not be filled if you have no unit that matches it.

**10.4.5 Use what you have.** If a place is still open and any uncovered unit could fill it, you must fill it. You are **not** required to find the arrangement that saves the most units. Where a unit could go into either a narrow pool or a broad one, that is your call, and a bad call is allowed to cost you.

**10.4.6 Anything left over routs.** Every unit with no place removes itself from the board, all at once (§9.3.3).

**10.4.7 Pay for them together.** Add up the Morale of everything that just routed and discard that many cards. If you cannot, you lose (§11.2).

> _Your cards hold: Manipular Legion ×2, formation ×1, generic ×1 — four places in three pools._
> _Your board has five units: 3 Manipular Legions, 1 Libyan Spearmen (formation), 1 Velites._
>
> _Five units, four places. Something is going to break; the only question is what._
>
> _The two Legion places take two Legions. The formation place can take the third Legion or the Spearmen — both qualify. The generic place takes whichever of those two is left. The Velites fit nowhere: they match no remaining pool, and they rout. Pay their Morale, which is nothing._
>
> _Note what you could have done instead. Put the Velites in the generic place and the Spearmen in the formation place, and it is the third Legion that routs — two Morale instead of none. That assignment is perfectly legal; §10.4.5 does not oblige you to find the better one._

> _From the sample deck. **Move**, **Socketed Pilum** and **Triplex Acies** each hold Manipular Legion ×2, and together make one pool of ×6. **Fearless Velites** holds Velites ×4 alone. Most other sample cards hold one generic place. No sample card holds a trait yet; the trait examples above are illustrative._

**10.4.8 What this costs you over a battle.** Nothing you commit and nothing you pay in Morale reduces your support, because those cards return. Only burning does, and you burn exactly one card per rally. An army's capacity to hold itself together therefore declines slowly, predictably, and entirely as a consequence of how often you have needed to rally.

## 11. Ending the game

Your hand is your army's will to fight. The game ends the instant it gives out. There is no grace period.

**11.1 An empty hand loses at once.** The moment your hand is empty, you **lose**. It does not matter how many cards wait in your played pile or discard: an army with nothing in hand has nothing left to fight with. That includes playing your last card in Phase 1 and committing your last card (§8).<sup>[B8](#b8)</sup>

**11.2 A cost you cannot survive.** If you must discard cards for Morale (§9.3) and the number owed is **equal to or greater than** the cards in your hand, you **lose immediately**. Paying with your last card is already a loss, which is §11.1 applied: you must survive the payment, not merely make it. A Morale debt of zero costs nothing.

**11.3 Both at once.** If both players' hands become empty at the same moment, which can only happen when both play their last card in Phase 1, the player who **held initiative going into the round** wins, since the cards were committed before they were revealed.<sup>[B8](#b8)</sup>

**11.4 No units left.** A player with no units on the board **loses immediately**, however many cards they hold. This will almost never come up, and it is not there for the cases where it does: it closes the door on feeding your own army to the enemy so that you can go on cycling a small hand indefinitely.<sup>[B9](#b9)</sup>

**11.5** There is no other way to win: no points, no objectives, no territory. Wiping out an army does end the game (§11.4), but as an accident rather than a plan. Long before that, the fighting has usually cost your opponent the cards they needed.

**11.6 An empty hand is never safe.** Before you play, commit or pay, count what will be left in your hand. If the answer is none, you lose. Rallying at Cleanup (§10.3) is how you get your cards back, so rally while you still have some.

## 12. Player aid

### 12.1 The round at a glance

```
@@FIG phases@@
1  PLAY CARDS        both choose face down → reveal → low initiative wins
                     (tie: current holder keeps it)

2  MOVE COMMANDERS   first player goes first · up to 4 spaces · no enemy spaces

3  ISSUE COMMANDS    first player: issue all, then resolve all
                     then second player: issue all, then resolve all
                     · movement orders may create engagements
                     · rear = defender routs now
                     · flank = defender wheels
                     · front = defender may fall back if faster

4  RESOLVE MELEE     first player picks the order
                     every engagement fights · both sides strike at once
                     results carried out by stage: retreats, reverses, routs
                     · first player's unit first within each stage

5  CLEANUP           in-play cards → played pile
                     first player: rally? → burn 1 → take back
                                        → assign units to support → leftovers rout
                     then second player: same
```

### 12.2 Quick tables

**Initiative** — card values run 1 to 4. Lower wins; tie goes to the current holder.

**Movement costs**

|                                   | Cost                     |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| One step, orthogonal or diagonal  | 1 Speed                  |
| One turn: 45° either way, or 180° | 1 Flexibility            |
| Passing through a friendly unit   | combined Flexibility ≥ 6 |

**Engagement type** — engaging unit's facing vs. defender's facing

| Same or 45° off                   | 90° off                                 | Opposite                                     |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Rear** — defender routs at once | **Flank** — defender wheels, no retreat | **Front** — defender may fall back if faster |

**Attack value** — Attack + Round Effect + Command modifiers + committed card + support.

**Melee support**

| Fighting unit is in supporter's… |        |
| -------------------------------- | ------ |
| front spaces                     | **+2** |
| flanking spaces                  | **+1** |
| back spaces                      | —      |

Supporter must be adjacent, unengaged, not behind the fighter, and not across a blocked diagonal (both side spaces held by enemies).

**Ranged support** — +1 per supporter, flat. A supporter must have been ordered by the same command, must be able to make the shot itself, and spends its own shot to support. The target must be unengaged.

**Combat results** — retreat, then reverse, then rout; in a melee, both retreats, then both reverses, then both routs

| Retreat                | Reverse                             | Rout                |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| smallest backward move | turn 180° (not while still engaged) | removed; pay Morale |

A unit that will rout can ignore its own retreat and reverse. A forced retreat with nowhere to go becomes a rout. In each stage of a melee the first player's unit goes first.

**Commitments** — +1 to each stat the card names, always optional. Each moment accepts only some stats:

| Before moving      | Front-engagement defence | Shooting / being shot     | Melee           |
| ------------------ | ------------------------ | ------------------------- | --------------- |
| Speed, Flexibility | Speed, Flexibility       | Attack or Range / Defence | Attack, Defence |

**You lose when** your hand is empty at any moment, whether you played, committed or paid away your last card, or when you have no units left on the board. If both hands empty at once, initiative wins.

## 13. Index of awkward situations

Things that come up, in the order players tend to hit them.

**"Can I turn and then charge in the same order?"** Yes. Turn (1 Flexibility), step, and turn again after the step if you have Flexibility left. Turns and steps can come in any order. §5.2.2

**"Can I turn twice on the spot?"** Yes, if you have the Flexibility. Each turn costs 1, so two 45° turns wheel the unit 90°. §5.2.2

**"My unit has Flexibility 0."** It cannot turn, ever, under any circumstances short of a card giving it +1. It also cannot be ordered to make a front engagement unless it already happens to be squared up to the target. §5.2.3, §6.2.3

**"Can I walk around their flank and hit them in the back?"** Not in one order. A rear engagement requires that you were _already_ behind them when the move began. §6.2.2

**"They have a unit in my way and I'm faster."** You can pass through friendlies if the combined Flexibility is 6 or more. You can never pass through an enemy, and you can never pass through a space where two units are engaged. §5.4

**"Both units routed each other in melee."** Yes, that happens. Melee is simultaneous in strength; the routs are carried out first player's unit first, and each owner pays Morale. §7.2.1, §7.5.6

**"My unit has to retreat but it's against the board edge."** It routs. §7.5, §9.1.6

**"There's an enemy behind my unit — can it still retreat?"** Yes, unless that enemy is right at your back, unengaged, and facing your unit. Then the retreat is cut off entirely. §9.1.5

**"Can I retreat further than necessary to get somewhere useful?"** No. A retreat is always the smallest legal backward move: fewest turns, then fewest steps. §9.1.3

**"My unit was hit hard enough to reverse, but it didn't turn."** It was still sharing a space with an enemy when its turn came. Only a unit that has fallen back, or whose enemy has gone, turns about. §7.5.5

**"Can my archers shoot through my own line?"** Yes, for now. Line of sight is not yet in the game, so anything in the forward arc and within Range can be shot. §4.3.11

**"Can my archers shoot at a unit that is already engaged?"** No. The target must be alone in its space. §7.4.2

**"Can my archers shoot while engaged?"** No. §4.3.11

**"Do my archers shoot automatically?"** No. Shooting only happens when a Shoot command orders it. A round in which you play a Move card is a round in which nobody shoots. §4.3.11

**"Can I command the same unit twice?"** No — once per round, whether alone or as part of a line. §4.3.6

**"Can a line include a unit I already commanded?"** No, and the line stops there rather than jumping over it. §4.3.6

**"How far does a line reach from my general?"** The end you name first must be within inspiration range. The rest of the line need only match the trait and type restrictions — it can run right off into the distance. §4.3.5

**"My card says 4 units. Do I have to order four?"** No. It is a maximum. You may order fewer, or none. §4.3.2

**"Do the command's modifiers last into the melee phase?"** Yes, for the whole round, for the units that were actually commanded. §4.3.8

**"Does the round effect apply to units I didn't command?"** Yes — that is exactly what distinguishes it from the command's modifiers. It applies to any of your units that satisfy _its_ restrictions. §4.3.7

**"Can I commit the card I played this round?"** No. Only cards in hand. §8.6

**"Do committed cards come back?"** Yes, on your next rally, along with everything else in your discard pile. Only the one card burnt during a rally is gone forever. §10.3

**"Can I play or commit my last card?"** You can, and you lose on the spot. An empty hand is an instant loss. Keep a card back. §11.1, §11.6

**"Can I rally with an empty hand?"** No. An empty hand has already lost the game. You can rally with a single card left: your played pile always holds at least this round's card to burn. §10.3, §11.1<sup>[B8](#b8)</sup>

**"If I never rally, do I ever lose units to the support check?"** No. The support check happens only after a rally. Declining to rally is a way to keep units on the board with a hand too thin to justify them — for exactly as long as you can afford to hold cards. §10.3.4

**"Does my opponent's rally affect my units?"** No. Each player checks only their own. §10.4

**"Two of my units both qualify for the last support place."** Your choice, and you are not required to make the choice that saves the most units. §10.4.5

**"I have exactly as many cards as the Morale I owe."** You lose. You must survive the payment, not merely make it. §11.2

**"Does a Defence commitment protect my unit in a melee?"** Yes: +1 to all three of its thresholds for that fight. It does nothing to the enemy's blow. §8.5<sup>[B3](#b3)</sup>

## 14. Glossary

**Adjacent** — orthogonally or diagonally touching; up to eight spaces. §2.4.1

**Arc** — the widening cone in front of a unit, used for shooting. §2.4.3

**Attack value** — the total a unit brings to one combat: Attack plus modifiers plus support. §7.1

**Back spaces** — the three spaces behind a unit. §2.3.2

**Burn** — to remove a card from the game permanently. Happens once per rally. §10.3

**Commander** — the general piece. No facing, no combat value; the origin of inspiration range. §4.2

**Commit** — to discard a card from hand for +1 to the stat it names, for one action. Always optional. §8

**Discard pile** — cards spent on commitments and Morale. Returns entirely on a rally. §10.1

**Engagement** — two enemy units locked in the same space. Created in Phase 3, fought in Phase 4. §6

**Flanking spaces** — the two spaces directly left and right of a unit. §2.3.2

**Flexibility** — how many times a unit may change facing in one move. Also governs passing through friends. §5.1

**Front spaces** — the three spaces a unit faces. §2.3.2

**In play** — the card you revealed this round. Not in hand and cannot be committed. §4.1.6

**Initiative** — acting first. Won by the lower card value; kept on a tie. §4.1

**Inspiration range** — how far from your commander a command or round effect reaches. §4.3.4

**Line** — a contiguous rank of 1–8 friendly units standing shoulder to shoulder, all facing the same direction or its opposite. §4.3.5

**Morale** — how many cards a unit's rout costs its owner. Also the army-building floor. §3.1.6, §9.3

**Played pile** — cards played in previous rounds, awaiting a rally. §10.1

**Pool** — all the support places your cards grant for one kind of unit, added together. §10.4.3

**Rally** — burn one card from the played pile, take everything else back, then check support. §10.3

**Reverse** — turned 180° in place. §9.2

**Rout** — removed from the battle; pay Morale. §9.3

**Round Effect** — a standing modifier from your in-play card, applying to qualifying units whether commanded or not. §4.3.7

**Support (combat)** — the bonus adjacent friends lend to a fight. §7.3

**Support (unit)** — the places your cards offer to keep units on the board, filled by you after a rally. A unit with no place routs. §10.4

**Threshold** — Retreat, Reverse, or Rout. Met when the attack value equals or exceeds it. Rout is applied first, then Retreat, then Reverse. §7.5

**Traits** — the groups cards use to name units: _formation_, _phalanx_, _javelin_, _cavalry_ and _beast_ in this roster. Other armies will use _melee_, _missile_, _shock_, _chariot_, _skirmisher_ and _warband_. §10.4.2

# APPENDIX — WHERE THE DIGITAL EDITION DIFFERS

Each entry is keyed to a mark in the rules. It says what the rule is, what the rules engine does instead, and what would close the gap. The entries come in four kinds:

- **Code lags rule:** the rule is settled and the engine has not caught up.
- **Bug:** the engine contradicts its own rule, or crashes.
- **Code and rule disagree:** both are deliberate and they conflict. One must give.
- **Rule open:** the design itself is not settled, and the engine has picked a reading for now.

"Probe" means the behaviour was run against the engine on 2026-11-08; otherwise it was read. Paths are under `src/domain/`.

## A — Board, setup, orders, movement, engagement

<a id="a1"></a>**A1. Engaged units are offered for orders, then skipped.** _Code lags rule._

- **Rule:** §4.3.4: an engaged unit cannot be ordered. §4.3.5: a line runs past an engaged unit, which is not itself commanded. That is the author's intent; Draft 3 §16.13 left it open.
- **Code does:** Issue-time eligibility never checks engagement, so an engaged unit is offered alone, as a line end and as a line member (`legality/choiceOptions/issueCommand/getLegalUnitsForIssueCommand.ts`; `getLegalLineEndsForIssueCommand.ts`, `canJoin`). Movement skips it at resolve (`getLegalMoveUnits.ts`; filter added in `778f05c`), so a "units" order can be spent on it for nothing. Probe: an engaged unit at E-5 was offered at issue and refused at resolve. A unit engaged inside a rank joins the line and is skipped at resolve, which is close to the rule for a rank. Offering it alone, or as a line end, is not.
- **To settle:** Filter engaged units out of single-unit orders and line ends at issue time. Decide whether an engaged unit in a rank still counts toward the line's eight units.

<a id="a2"></a>**A2. One Flexibility reaches any facing; the rule is 45° or 180°.** _Code lags rule._

- **Rule:** §5.2.1: a turn is 45° either way, or 180° about. (Author decision 2026-11-08; `AUDIT.md`, Movement item 1.)
- **Code does:** From the start and after each step, any of the seven other facings costs one Flexibility (`legality/unitMovement/exploreUnitMoves.ts`). A Flexibility-1 phalanx can therefore wheel 90° on the spot.
- **Also:** The engine explores one turn at the start and one after each step, so it offers no second turn in a row. §5.2.2 lets turns and steps come in any order, including two turns on the spot. This is read from `exploreUnitMoves`, not probed.
- **To settle:** Restrict the facings `exploreUnitMoves` offers per spend to the two 45° neighbours and the reverse, and let turns repeat in any order within the Flexibility available. Read retreat turning (A14) against the same rule.

<a id="a3"></a>**A3. Terrain, elevation, water and line of sight are planned and absent.** _Code lags rule._ (Draft §16.7; `AUDIT.md` item 3.)

- **Rule:** Ground is bare and transparent; shots ignore everything between firer and target.
- **Code does:** Board spaces carry terrain, elevation and water fields that no rule reads (`src/domain/entities/board/`). `getCurrentUnitStat` has a terrain step "not yet implemented; this step adds nothing" (`queries/unit/getCurrentUnitStat.ts`). Shooting checks only the arc and range (`legality/choiceOptions/performRangedAttack/canUnitRangedAttackTarget.ts`, "No line-of-sight check"). `AUDIT.md` says the terrain rules "have firmed up" but are not written down in the code.
- **To settle:** Write the terrain and line-of-sight rules, then revisit Range, the forward arc and the value of fast troops. Until then, §2.1.4 and the §4.3.11 edge case stand.

<a id="a4"></a>**A4. Commanders can share a space with the enemy commander.** _Rule open._

- **Rule:** More than one thing may stand in a space if one is a commander. A commander may not enter a space holding an enemy **unit**.
- **Code does:** Commander movement only refuses enemy units, so it may end on, or pass through, a space holding the enemy commander (`legality/commanderMovement/exploreCommanderMoves.ts`, `exploreCommanderMoves`, `hasEnemyUnit`). Nothing refers to two commanders in one space.
- **To settle:** Allow it, or forbid ending a move there. Related: the engine never defeats a commander (A15), so a shared space has no consequence today.

<a id="a5"></a>**A5. Deployment is a placeholder, and White deploys first.** _Rule open._ (Draft §16.8.)

- **Rule:** A flat three-row belt, symmetrical, whole army placed at once.
- **Code does:** The belt is "KISS provisional" and "per-mode / scenario zones will replace this later" (`legality/choiceOptions/setupUnits/getSetupZoneCoordinates.ts`). White sets up first and Black second, whatever the initiative; the comment calls the asymmetry deliberate: seeing the opponent deploy against striking first (`expected/expectedEvent/byPhase/getExpectedSetupUnitsEvent.ts`). Each player places all units and the commander in one go, with any facing, and the commander's space may repeat a unit's (`validation/playerChoice/isValidSetupUnitsEvent.ts`). Draft 3 never said who deploys first.
- **To settle:** Decide whether deploying first against holding initiative is the intended trade, and say so in the rulebook when the setup rules are rebuilt.

<a id="a6"></a>**A6. Inspiration range: "none" is −1, "0" means the commander's own space, and nearly every sample card prints a range.** _Rule open._ (Draft §16.3.)

- **Rule:** A range of none means no restriction; 0 means the commander's space. Draft 3 said most commands print no range.
- **Code does:** A negative value (−1 is the lowest the schema allows) means no restriction; 0 or more is a range; a missing commander fails every range (`entities/card/restrictions.ts`; `legality/choiceOptions/issueCommand/getLegalUnitsForIssueCommand.ts`, `unitMatchesInspirationRange`; `queries/unit/getCurrentUnitStat.ts`). No sample card uses −1: every command and round effect prints 0 to 4, most commands a 1, so a unit must stand next to the commander to be ordered (`sampleValues/tempCommandCards.ts`). Two round effects print 0 (Brace, Noble Cavalry). The check is made once at issue time for commands, but each time a stat is read for round effects, from wherever the unit then stands. For a unit walking into an enemy the stat is read from its **starting** space (see A11). The same range test is written twice (`TODO(composition)` in `getCurrentUnitStat.ts`); the copies agree today.
- **To settle:** Decide the printed convention (blank versus −1 versus 0) and whether any shipped card is meant to be unrestricted; then keep one copy of the range test.

<a id="a7"></a>**A7. Which eight units a long rank offers depends on where you start; the line rule exists twice.** _Code and rule disagree._

- **Rule:** A line is 1 to 8 units, named by its two ends.
- **Code does:** The engine builds the contiguous rank around the start unit, stretching first to the start's left (up to seven units) and then to its right until eight are gathered, so in a rank longer than eight some end units cannot be named from some starts (`getLegalLineEndsForIssueCommand.ts`, `getLineSegmentFromStart`; checked again in `validation/playerChoice/isValidIssueCommandEvent.ts`). A second line rule, which lists **every** eight-unit window that contains a unit, is exported but called by no one (`queries/line/getLinesFromUnit.ts`; `TODO(composition)`). `isValidLine` (`legality/choiceOptions/issueCommand/isValidLine.ts`) is a third statement of the shape.
- **To settle:** §4.3.5 says you choose which eight, which is the window rule. Keep `getLinesFromUnit`'s windows (or equivalent), retire the greedy cap, and keep one statement of the line shape.

<a id="a8"></a>**A8. The mover never gets to commit.** _Code lags rule._

- **Rule:** §4.3.10: before a unit moves, its owner may commit for +1 Speed or Flexibility; that Speed also counts in a front-engagement contest (§8.4.1). Draft 3 says so, and the engine has a built branch for it.
- **Code does:** The destination is chosen first, and `applyMoveUnitEvent` records the mover's commitment as declined (`transforms/stateTransitions/applyChoices/applyMoveUnitEvent.ts`, since `d455135`). The pending-commit branch in `getLegalCommitToMovementEvents.ts` and `getExpectedMovementResolutionEvent.ts` is unreachable, though its tests still build it. Only the front-engagement defender is ever asked.
- **To settle:** Ask for the commitment before the destination, and read it in the move search (`getLegalUnitMoves`, `exploreUnitMoves`) as well as the Speed contest.

<a id="a9"></a>**A9. A movement order carries a flag to bring the commander along; nothing uses it.** _Rule open._

- **Rule:** None; the commander moves only in Phase 2.
- **Code does:** The `moveUnit` choice has a `moveCommander` flag, stored in the movement state and never read (`events/playerChoices/moveUnit.ts`; `applyMoveUnitEvent.ts`; `game/substeps/commandResolution/movementResolutionSubstep.ts`).
- **To settle:** Decide whether a commander may ride with a unit. If not, remove the flag; if so, write the rule (range, engagement, facing).

<a id="a10"></a>**A10. Units and the "blocked diagonal" test do not agree with commanders and support.** _Code and rule disagree._

- **Rule:** §2.4.4: two enemies on the flanking orthogonals block a diagonal.
- **Code does:** Commanders and melee support use exactly that (`queries/board/diagonalIsClear.ts`; called from `exploreCommanderMoves.ts` and `getMeleeSupportValue.ts`). Units use a stricter test: at least one of the two orthogonals must be passable by that unit, so an enemy, an engaged space, or a friend too stiff to pass through counts against it (`legality/unitMovement/checkDiagonalMove.ts`). Two stiff friends can therefore block a diagonal for a unit, and cannot for a commander or for support. The destination must also be passable by the same test, which forbids ending a diagonal step on an enemy (see A12). A comment on `diagonalIsClear` is marked `TODO(misleading)`.
- **To settle:** Decide whether units should use the enemy-only rule, or whether commanders and support should use the stricter one.

<a id="a11"></a>**A11. The mover is not in the shared space until the engagement is set up; its Speed is measured from where it began.** _Rule open._ (`AUDIT.md`, "Movement (not settled)" item 2.)

- **Rule:** An engagement exists the moment a unit ends a step in the enemy's space; Speeds are compared as they stand.
- **Code does:** On a move into an enemy space the mover stays on its start space while its target is stored beside it; the move is applied only when the engagement is set up (`applyMoveUnitEvent.ts`, "the mover has not moved yet"). Any stat that depends on position, such as a round effect's inspiration range, is therefore read from the start space during the front-engagement Speed comparison. No canonical path is stored for animation.
- **To settle:** Decide whether the Speed race should read the destination, and keep a path on the mover as the audit asks.

<a id="a12"></a>**A12. A diagonal step can never end on an enemy.** _Bug._

- **Rule:** §6.2.1: engaging from a flanking space is always allowed, straight or diagonal, and a diagonal step passes the usual corner test (§5.4.5). Nothing about the engagement is special for being diagonal.
- **Code does:** For a diagonal step the move search first asks whether the unit could pass _through_ the target, which an enemy never allows (`legality/unitMovement/exploreUnitMoves.ts`; `checkDiagonalMove.ts`, whose own comment says the caller should check the end space). Probe: diagonal-facing defenders were reached only from the rear or at 135°, never in the flank. No test engages diagonally.
- **To settle:** Check the destination of a diagonal step with `canMoveInto`/`canEngageEnemy`, not `canMoveThrough`.

<a id="a13"></a>**A13. The squaring-up turn is checked, never made; a 135° approach crashes.** _Bug._

- **Rule:** §6.2.3: a front approach ends facing directly opposite, spending 1 Flexibility if needed. §6.3.1 then always classifies.
- **Code does:** `canEngageEnemy.ts` only checks that 1 Flexibility remains; the turn is not applied. Against a diagonal-facing defender a step from an orthogonal front space arrives at 135°, which `isEngagementFromFront` and its siblings do not cover, and `generateStartEngagementEvent` throws (`TODO(wrong)` in `queries/engagement/isEngagementFromFront.ts`). Probe: Flexibility 2 against a SW-facing defender offered 28 such approaches. With Flexibility 0 only rear approaches exist, so no crash. Orthogonal defenders are unaffected. Intent: test titles in `canEngageEnemy.test.ts` ("has flexibility to rotate to face opposite").
- **To settle:** Apply the turn as part of the engaging move (and spend the point), so 135° cannot arise.

<a id="a14"></a>**A14. Retreat turning after the first step is unrestricted.** _Rule open._

- **Rule:** §9.1.4: a retreat may turn at most 45° at its start. The rule is silent on turns after a step.
- **Code does:** Probe: the start turn is limited to the two adjacent facings, but after a step the search allows any facing. Retreats are the minimal move (fewest turns, then fewest steps; confirmed), so this rarely shows.
- **To settle:** Decide whether later turns follow §5.2.1 (45°/180°), the 45° retreat limit, or are forbidden.

<a id="a15"></a>**A15. Commanders are never defeated.** _Code lags rule._

- **Rule:** §4.2.8: a commander is defeated if it stands in a space where a unit routs in combat, and not when a unit routs at the support check.
- **Code does:** The game state carries `lostCommanders`, and `addCommanderToLostCommanders` exists, but nothing in production calls it: a rout never touches a commander (`game/gameState/`; `transforms/pureTransforms/commanders/addCommanderToLostCommanders.ts`). Read, not probed. What a lost commander should change is not written anywhere.
- **To settle:** Decide what defeat costs, then call the transform from the combat rout path only, keeping the support-check rout out of it.

## B — Combat, commitments, cards, endgame

<a id="b1"></a>**B1. The target of a shot is never offered a Defence card.** _Code lags rule._

- **Rule:** §4.3.11, §7.4.5: the defender may commit for Defence. (Author decision 2026-11-08.)
- **Code does:** `generateResolveRangedAttackEvent` would apply it, but `getLegalCommitToRangedAttackEvents` offers both sides the same list (Range, Attack, Flexibility), so only a two-stat card that happens to name Defence could ever protect a target.
- **To settle:** Give attacker and defender separate accepted-stat lists: Attack for the attacker, Defence for the defender.

<a id="b2"></a>**B2. Attack value is never clamped at zero.** _Code and rule disagree._

- **Rule:** 7.1.4, an attack value cannot be reduced below zero.
- **Code does:** sums base, modifiers and support with no floor. `getCurrentUnitStat` returns `baseStat + totalModifier` and `applyAttackValue` compares the raw sum. A negative value fails every threshold, including a printed Reverse of 0, whereas a value of exactly 0 meets it. (`src/domain/queries/unit/getCurrentUnitStat.ts`, `src/domain/queries/attack/applyAttackValue.ts:14`)
- **To settle:** either add the floor (so a negative attack behaves as 0, and still reverses a 0-threshold unit), or drop the sentence. Settle together with B4.

<a id="b3"></a>**B3. Defence commitments do nothing in melee.** _Code lags rule._

- **Rule:** §8.5: a Defence commitment raises its own unit's thresholds by 1 for that combat, in melee as in ranged.
- **Code does:** `generateResolveMeleeEvent` passes no defending modifiers to `applyAttackValue`, so a melee Defence card is discarded for nothing (`procedures/resolveAttack/generateResolveMeleeEvent.ts`; `TODO(wrong)`; unchanged since `f69a8d1`). The ranged path does pass them, and the old rulebook lists Defence as a melee commit.
- **To settle:** Pass each side's own commitment as that unit's defending modifiers; add a spec with a committed Defence card.

<a id="b4"></a>**B4. Reverse threshold of 0.** _Rule open._ (Draft 16.2; still current.)

- **Rule:** 7.5.1, a threshold is met when the attack value is equal or greater, so 0 is always met.
- **Code does:** Velites, Numidian Skirmishers and Numidian Cavalry store Reverse 0 and the data comment says dashes are stored as 0, so any non-negative attack meets it. Their thresholds read 1 / 0 / 3, the only non-ascending set. Effect is mostly hidden by 7.5.5 (a unit still engaged does not turn), so it bites on ranged hits and after a retreat. (`src/domain/sampleValues/tempUnits.ts:25,105,125`; `src/domain/queries/attack/applyAttackValue.ts:44-46`)
- **To settle:** decide whether 0 means "never reverses" (needs a clause or a sentinel) or a real threshold (then print it honestly). Interacts with B2.

<a id="b5"></a>**B5. A rout forced by a blocked retreat cannot be paid.** _Bug._

- **Rule:** 7.5 edge case and 9.1.6, a unit that must retreat and cannot routs and its owner pays Morale (9.3).
- **Code does:** the engine opens the rout (`triggerRoutFromRetreat`) and `findRoutLocation` can find it, but the rout sits inside the retreat and `updateRoutState` has no case for writing it. For an attack-apply or front-engagement parent it throws, so `applyChooseRoutDiscardEvent` cannot pay it. A zero penalty also closes the nested rout without closing its retreat, which `getExpectedRetreatEvent` then refuses. Marked `TODO(wrong)`. (`src/domain/transforms/pureTransforms/sequencing/updateRoutState.ts:26-37`; AUDIT.md `transforms`)
- **Probe:** a unit at the board edge hit to Retreat runs `openRetreat → triggerRoutFromRetreat → resolveRout → chooseRoutDiscard`; the discard throws "No rout state found in attack apply state" (`updateRoutState.ts:113`). With Morale 0, `getExpectedEvent` throws instead. The game stalls either way.
- **To settle:** a failing spec for a retreat-held rout, then write through `updateRetreatRoutState`. Until then the rulebook states the intent; the engine would crash at the payment.

<a id="b6"></a>**B6. A card with two commit stats applies both.** _Rule open._ (Draft 8.2 offered a choice.)

- **Rule:** draft 8.2, "where one does, choose as you commit."
- **Code does:** `modifiersFromCompletedCommitment` turns every stat on the card into +1, and the events offer no choice between them. A card may name one or two stats (`min(1).max(2)`). No sample card names two. (`src/domain/queries/card/modifiersFromCompletedCommitment.ts:10-20`; `src/domain/entities/card/commandCard.ts:64`)
- **To settle:** decide between "both apply" (as written in 8.2 now) and "choose one", then make the legal-events list and the modifier query agree. Note too that a two-stat card committed where one stat is not accepted still lends the other.

<a id="b7"></a>**B7. Flexibility and Range commitments are offered and never read.** _Rule open._

- **Rule:** §8.4 lists the stats each moment accepts. Flexibility counts before a move (once A8 is fixed). Range widens a shot's forward arc by one (§4.3.11, committed before the target is chosen). Flexibility has no use in a shot or a melee until the terrain rules land.
- **Code does:** Commitments are read only in the front-engagement Speed contest, the ranged attack value and target thresholds, and the melee attack value (`procedures/movement/generateResolveEngageRetreatOptionEvent.ts`; `resolveAttack/`). The engine still accepts Range and Flexibility in shooting, and Flexibility in melee (`getLegalCommitTo*Events.ts`).
- **To settle:** Make the engine read +1 Range when it builds the forward arc, which means offering the commitment before the target is chosen. Let a Range commitment count when deciding whether a unit has a target at all. Leave Flexibility inert in a shot or a melee until terrain, or stop offering it there.

<a id="b8"></a>**B8. Both hands empty at once is a draw in the engine; the rule gives it to initiative.** _Code lags rule._

- **Rule:** §11.1: an empty hand loses at once, so playing or committing your last card loses. §11.3: if both hands empty at the same moment, the player who held initiative going into the round wins (author decision 2026-11-08).
- **Code does:** `getExpectedEvent` asks `getGameOverWinner` before every event, so playing or committing your last card ends the game at once, as the rule says (`expected/expectedEvent/getExpectedEvent.ts`). Both empty is scored a draw (`queries/gameOver/getWinnerFromEmptyHands.ts`). Probe: an empty hand at the rally choice yields `gameOver`.
- **To settle:** Give the tie to the player who held initiative going into the round.

<a id="b9"></a>**B9. No-units-left is not a loss.** _Code lags rule._

- **Rule:** §11.4: a player with no units loses. First stated in Draft 3; the old book did not have it, so confidence in intent is moderate.
- **Code does:** `getGameOverWinner` has two arms, empty hand and unpayable rout; nothing counts units (`queries/gameOver/getGameOverWinner.ts`).
- **To settle:** Confirm the rule, then add the arm.

<a id="b10"></a>**B10. Melee results are carried out unit by unit; the rule is stage by stage.** _Code lags rule._

- **Rule:** §7.5.6: both retreats, then both reverses, then both routs, with the first player's unit first in each stage.
- **Code does:** The engine carries out the first player's unit's results in full (retreat, reverse, rout), then the second player's. A probe on 2026-11-08 confirmed that order; the stage order is read, not probed. The two differ when one unit's retreat would end the other's engagement before its reverse: a first-player unit that only reverses is judged before the second player's unit falls back, so it is still engaged and does not turn, where the rule has it turn.
- **To settle:** Apply melee results in the stage order, and check the retreat, reverse and rout generators against it (`procedures/defenseResult/`).

## Changes from Draft 3

These were errors in the earlier books, not in the engine. The engine's values are deliberate, and this book now prints them.

- **Army limits** (§3.1.3): cost cap 200 / 300 / 500 including cards, Morale floor 10 / 20 / 30, up to 12 copies of a type; Tutorial has no cost, Morale or spread limit (`ruleValues/ruleValues.ts`, `legality/army/`; set in `48aba02`). Draft 3's first-game armies were short of the Morale floor; Part I now fields 4 + 1.
- **Passing through a friend** (§5.4.2) needs combined Flexibility **6**, not 4 (`MIN_FLEXIBILITY_THRESHOLD`; `48aba02`). Roster Flexibility values were raised to match (§15).
- **Traits** are _javelin, melee, missile, shock, beast, chariot, cavalry, skirmisher, phalanx, warband, formation_ (`ruleValues/traits.ts`).
- **"The spaces behind"** a unit is the whole half of the board beyond its back rank, not a wedge (`queries/board/areas/getSpacesBehind.ts`) (§2.3.3).
- **Shooting into a melee** is forbidden (§4.3.11). Draft 3 was silent; the engine has required a lone target since `cd05333`. Confirmed as intended 2026-11-08.
- **An unresolved engagement is fought again next round** (§7.2.3). Confirmed by probe.
- **The support example** in the oldest rulebook (U at E-5 facing East, supporter at D-4) put the supporter in a back space, where it gives nothing (`queries/attack/getMeleeSupportValue.ts`). §7.3.2 replaces it.

## Engine findings with no rule at stake

These do not change how the game plays today, but they are worth the next read of the code.

- **Faults inside movement checks read as "illegal move".** `canEngageEnemy`, `canMoveInto`, `canMoveThrough` and `isLegalMove` catch everything and return false (`legality/unitMovement/`; `TODO(wrong)` in each). `isValidLine` reports the fault instead, which is the shape the others should take.
- **Rally offered with an empty played pile, then throws** (`getLegalChooseRallyEvent.ts`, `generateResolveRallyEvent.ts`). Unreachable in play, because the round's card is always in the pile by then (probe-confirmed throw on a constructed state). Offer only "no rally" when the pile is empty.
- **`getNextMeleeRoutState` returns a rout already paid**, and has no production caller (`queries/sequencing/combatOutcomes/rout/`; `TODO(wrong)`).
- **`getNextStepForResolveRally` defaults any non-resolve step to complete** (`queries/sequencing/rallyResolution/cleanupPhase/`; `TODO(wrong)`). Callers guard it today.

## Open design questions

Not inconsistencies. Decisions the design has not yet made.

- **What commander defeat costs.** A commander is defeated where a unit routs in combat (§4.2.8). The book does not yet say what that costs the player beyond reaching no unit with an inspiration range. (Draft 3 §16.6.)
- **The rally burn is random.** It is the game's only randomness and the only step that needs a physical shuffle. A chosen or oldest-first burn would make rally timing a cleaner decision. (Draft 3 §16.12.)
- **Support assignment is your own call** (§10.4.5). You must fill open places, but need not find the arrangement that saves the most units. Confirm that a poor assignment is meant to be allowed to cost you.
- **Who chooses among equally short retreats** (§9.1.3). The retreating player does, today. Confirm.
